# Telescopic must report what unbiasedness hides

Everything telescopic needs mathematically is published and can be pinned down to the index, yet no maintained package implements it in any language, so the work ahead is engineering and candour rather than new theory. The quantity to implement is Jacob, O'Leary and Atchadé's lag-L time-averaged estimator with the floor/ceil weights used by the R reference code and the 2024 review; the lag-L weight printed in the 2020 authors' reply is biased for L ≥ 2. Exact unbiasedness holds for every k ≤ m, but accuracy at a finite number of replicates does not: the paper's own bimodal example returned a 95% interval of [0.799, 0.816] for a true value of 0.421, so meeting-time tails, multimodality and the initial distribution π0 carry the real risk. The defensible gradient-free default is random-walk Metropolis with reflection-maximal proposals and a shared uniform, tuned on a pilot and then frozen (k at the 99% quantile of τ − L, then L = k and m = 10k); its meeting times grow roughly linearly up to a few hundred well-conditioned dimensions, and the couplings that scale further need gradients. The ensemble moves that emcee users expect cannot be coupled: the census's simulations found no contraction and zero meetings, so an ensemble belongs only in the pilot that sets π0 and the preconditioner. The reference code is a set of untested GPL R scripts, every Python port is abandoned and several declare meetings by tolerance or truncate silently, and BlackJAX's new coupled HMC explicitly stops short of meetings and estimators. Under deadlines, averaging whatever has finished is biased (z = +165 in the census's simulation), and Glynn and Heidelberger's per-worker rule removes the bias. Validation has to rest on exact algebra and finite-state expectations, because a one-index weight bug shifts the mean by about 0.03 and needs about four million replicates to show statistically. The specification should therefore centre on exact meeting flags, explicit censoring, per-replicate random streams, budget-safe estimators, tail diagnostics that warn loudly and a layered emcee/dynesty-style API; the open questions are a validated rule for the number of replicates, calibrated warning thresholds and valid intervals for quantiles, which telescopic will have to answer with its own evidence.

## The lag-L estimator is settled, except for one misprinted weight

Results attributed to "the census" below are the research team's own derivations, simulations and benchmarks, recorded with their setups in the research notes; they are design evidence, not published findings. Timings were measured on a 4-vCPU Intel Xeon VM at 2.10 GHz with Python 3.13.16, NumPy 2.5.3 and OpenBLAS 0.3.34.

### Exact meeting makes Glynn–Rhee truncation unnecessary

The package is named for a telescoping sum. Glynn and Rhee write a limit as a sum of differences Δ_k and estimate it without bias by truncating at an independent random level N and dividing each term by P(N ≥ k); for chains coupled through regeneration, the differences vanish after the coupling time, and they remark that "we may set N = ∞ a.s. in this setting" ([Glynn & Rhee](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.4302)). Jacob, O'Leary and Atchadé built a general method on this observation. They run a chain X and a copy Y lagged by one step, couple the two so that they meet exactly at a random time τ and stay together afterwards, and use E_π h = E h(X_k) + Σ_{t>k} {E h(X_t) − E h(Y_{t−1})}, whose terms vanish after τ. The estimator H_k = h(X_k) + Σ_{t=k+1}^{τ−1} {h(X_t) − h(Y_{t−1})} is then unbiased with no truncation distribution to tune ([Jacob, O'Leary & Atchadé](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)), and Douc, Jacob, Lee and Vats show that the Glynn–Rhee estimator is identical to H_0 under the lag-one initialisation ([Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691)). Random truncation applied directly to ergodic averages, as McLeish proposed, yields estimators that "tend to have either a large cost or a large variance", which is why exact meeting matters ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). The method sits between ordinary MCMC and perfect sampling: "only two chains have to be coupled for the proposed estimator to be unbiased", rather than coalescence from every state as in coupling from the past, and the output is an unbiased signed measure rather than exact draws ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). A randomised-truncation fallback is needed only for couplings that contract but never meet, which lies outside the published exact-meeting methods ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)).

### The floor/ceil weights are correct; the rejoinder's ceiling weight is not

The quantity to implement is the lag-L time-averaged estimator

$$H^{(L)}_{k:m}=\frac{1}{m-k+1}\Big[\sum_{t=k}^{m}h(X_t)+\sum_{t=k+L}^{\tau-1}\Big(\Big\lfloor\tfrac{t-k}{L}\Big\rfloor-\Big\lceil\tfrac{\max(L,\,t-m)}{L}\Big\rceil+1\Big)\big(h(X_t)-h(Y_{t-L})\big)\Big],$$

built by drawing X_0 and Y_0 independently from π0, advancing X alone for L steps, then drawing (X_t, Y_{t−L}) from the coupled kernel until X_t = Y_{t−L} and t ≥ m, with X_t = Y_{t−L} enforced for all t ≥ τ. The integer coefficient counts the pairs (l, j) with k ≤ l ≤ m, j ≥ 1 and l + jL = t in the definitional average (m − k + 1)⁻¹ Σ_l H_l, and the same coefficient appears in the R reference code, in Atchadé and Jacob's review (eq. 2.10) and in Douc et al. (eq. 3.6) ([H_bar.R](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/660c69824d3e31b109070b647578d14b045c33d5/R/H_bar.R); [Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851); [Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691)). At L = 1 it reduces to Jacob et al.'s weight min(1, (t − k)/(m − k + 1)); at m = k it gives the single-time lagged estimator of Biswas, Jacob and Vanetti; and at m = k + L − 1 every correction weight equals one, which is the estimator Vanetti and Doucet proposed in the discussion of the read paper ([Biswas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09971); [Vanetti & Doucet](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~doucet/discussion_VanettiDoucet_unbiasedMCMC.pdf)).

**The lag-L weight printed in the 2020 authors' reply, min{1, ⌈(l − k)/L⌉/(m − k + 1)}, is biased for L ≥ 2.** On a three-state chain with π(h) = 1.67857 and 2×10⁵ replicates per configuration, the census found that the floor/ceil weights averaged 1.6806 at (k, m, L) = (0, 0, 2) while the printed weight averaged 2.0607, and that the definitional average matched the floor/ceil formula to within 2×10⁻¹⁵ in every replicate ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md); [JRSSB article page](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/82/3/543/7056129)). The superseded coefficient survives in the R package only as commented-out C++ ([c_chains_to_measure.cpp](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/660c69824d3e31b109070b647578d14b045c33d5/src/c_chains_to_measure.cpp)). Two caveats apply. The census read the reply through an HTML transcription, so the typeset PDF must be checked before the discrepancy is called an erratum, and a second text extraction lost the rounding bracket altogether; a simplified floor form, min(1, ⌊(l − k)/L⌋/(m − k + 1)), matches the correct estimator only when τ ≤ m + L + 1 ([census check](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/diagnostics_practice.md)). Two existing ports hard-code the lag-1 weight, which does not generalise: ColCarroll's `couplings` accepts a `lag` argument but still applies lag-1 weights, and CoupledHMC.jl uses the lag-1 formula throughout ([metropolis_hastings.py](https://github.com/ColCarroll/couplings/blob/master/couplings/metropolis_hastings.py); [CoupledHMC.jl analysis.jl](https://github.com/chalk-lab/CoupledHMC.jl/blob/main/src/analysis.jl)).

The sources disagree on notation and on the meeting time, and the disagreements matter for tuning even though they do not change the estimate.

| Item | Jacob et al. 2020 | Biswas et al. 2019 | Atchadé & Jacob 2024 | Douc et al. | `unbiasedmcmc` (R) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last retained index | m | m | ℓ | ℓ | `m` |
| Meeting time | inf{t ≥ 1 : X_t = Y_{t−1}} | inf{t > L : X_t = Y_{t−L}} | inf{t ≥ L : X_t = Y_{t−L}} | inf{t > L : X_t = Y_{t−L}} | first t ≥ L + 1 at which the coupled kernel returns `identical` |
| Correction weight | min(1, (t − k)/(m − k + 1)) | through the inner sum's upper limit | v_t, including 1/(ℓ − k + 1) | integer v_t, divided separately | integer, divided at the end |
| Other meaning of m | none | none | moment index of h | moment index of h | none |

The meeting-time conventions differ only in whether X_L = Y_0 counts as a meeting, which has positive probability for discrete states, for MH chains that reject, and for atoms in π0. They never change H when the coupling is faithful, because every difference term vanishes once X_t = Y_{t−L}, but they change the recorded τ, the cost and every rule that tunes from quantiles of τ ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)). The R kernel flags a meeting only when proposals coincide and both chains accept, so a pair that is already equal and jointly rejects is recorded later than it met, which follows from its flag definition ([get_mh_kernels.R](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/master/R/get_mh_kernels.R)). Comparisons with the literature need conversions, since O'Leary's coupling tables use no lag at all ([O'Leary 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01790)).

### Efficiency approaches plain MCMC only when m dwarfs k and τ

**One replicate costs L + 2(τ − L) + max(0, m − τ) kernel units**, counting a coupled step as two units before meeting and one afterwards; at L = 1 this equals Jacob et al.'s 2(τ − 1) + max(1, m + 1 − τ), and the R driver returns the same quantity ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851); [coupled_chains.R](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/660c69824d3e31b109070b647578d14b045c33d5/R/coupled_chains.R)). The unit is an approximation: the R kernel skips the second log-density evaluation when proposals coincide, and an HMC step costs its leapfrog gradients, so telescopic should count evaluations and wall time directly ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)). Efficiency is the asymptotic inefficiency E[cost] × Var(H) of Glynn and Whitt, compared with the MCMC asymptotic variance v(P, h); as m grows, √(m − k + 1)(H_{k:m} − π(h)) converges to N(0, v(P, h)), and the loss relative to MCMC is approximately 1 + (k + E[τ])/(m − k) ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691)). Jacob et al.'s bimodal example shows the size of the effect: with mean τ of 20 and a 99% quantile of 105, inefficiency relative to V∞ fell from **1878.4 at k = 1, m = k to 2.9 at k = 100, m = 10k and 1.3 at k = 200, m = 10k** ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). With k at the 99% quantile, L = k and m = 10k, the loss cannot fall below about m/(m − k + 1) ≈ 1.11 before bias-correction terms are counted ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)). Lags matter most when k and m are small: in Vanetti and Doucet's random-walk example, the standard deviation of one estimate fell from 430 with lag 1 at (k, m) = (1, 10) to 66.7 with L = m − k, and the two coincided at 0.119 for (100, 1000) ([Vanetti & Doucet](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~doucet/discussion_VanettiDoucet_unbiasedMCMC.pdf)). The authors' reply calls lags L > 1 "always possible" and adds that users "might find lagged estimators with L > 1 to be safer in general" ([JRSSB reply](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)).

### Signed measures give unbiased histograms but only consistent quantiles

Each replicate defines a signed empirical measure with (m − k + 1) + 2 max(0, τ − k − L) atoms: weight 1/(m − k + 1) on X_k, …, X_m and ±v_t/(m − k + 1) on each pair (X_t, Y_{t−L}), summing to one ([Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691)); the review's atom count omits the factor 2 ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)). Applying the measure to any integrable h, indicators included, gives unbiased estimates, so histogram bins and CDF values are unbiased and test functions can be chosen after the run if the atoms are stored ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Three consequences shape the outputs. First, estimates can be negative or fall outside the range of h, and **negative weights can be reduced by raising k but never removed**, because no algorithm turns unbiased estimators of a non-negative quantity into non-negative unbiased ones (Jacob & Thiery 2015, cited by [Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). Second, the pooled CDF is not monotone, so quantiles are only consistent as R → ∞, and confidence intervals for them remain "an interesting area for future research" ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Third, nonlinear functionals need products of independent replicates: Douc et al.'s two-replicate variance estimator generalises to exact U-statistics over R replicates, for example π(h)² estimated by ā² − s²_a/R, whereas the pooled plug-in Σωh² − (Σωh)² is biased by −Var(ā) ([Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691); [census derivation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)). A general smooth g(π(h)) needs the randomised multilevel construction of Wang and Wang ([Wang & Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04808)). A weighted kernel density estimate is unbiased only for the smoothed density, and histogram bins should be half-open so that bin probabilities sum exactly to one ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)).

Coupled chains also estimate the MCMC asymptotic variance itself, which gives telescopic an efficiency report without long runs. Douc et al.'s UPAVE combines two signed measures with lag-0 coupled estimates of the Poisson-equation solution, is unbiased and converges at the Monte Carlo rate, where batch means converge at T^{−2/3}; on an AR(1) chain with φ = 0.99 and exact v = 10⁴, 10³ independent estimates costing about 5,250 transitions each averaged to a 95% bootstrap interval of [8049, 10417] ([Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691); [Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). Of the variance reductions proposed in the discussion, only lags became standard. Craiu and Meng's control variates gain little once L and m are large, and their leave-one-out coefficients make replicates dependent ([Craiu & Meng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12662)); Sherlock's Rao–Blackwellisation of MH coupling events cut the MSE by up to a factor of 30 at less than twice the cost, but the census could not obtain its formulas; and South, Nemeth and Oates's Stein control variates gave a median variance reduction of about 20 on a 302-dimensional logistic regression ([JRSSB discussion](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901); [South et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10496)).

## Unbiasedness holds at every k; accuracy at finite R does not

The guarantee rests on three published sets of assumptions. Jacob et al. assume a uniform 2 + η moment of h along the chain, geometric tails for τ and faithfulness, and conclude that H_{k:m} has expectation π(h), finite variance and finite expected cost for every 0 ≤ k ≤ m, without the constants needing to be known ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Middleton et al. relax the tails to P(τ > n) ≤ Kn^{−κ} with κ > 2(2/η + 1), which covers pseudo-marginal and exchange algorithms and requires κ just above 6 when η = 1 ([Middleton et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08691)). Douc et al. give the weakest form: if E_{π⊗π}[τ^κ] < ∞, h ∈ L^m(π) and dπ0/dπ ≤ M, then for any k ≤ ℓ and L ≥ 1 the estimator is unbiased with E|H|^p < ∞ whenever 1/p > 1/m + 1/κ, so **finite variance needs κ > 2 and m > 2κ/(κ − 2)** ([Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691)). The bounded-density condition rules out a Dirac π0 for continuous targets but allows a uniform distribution on any ball ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). For 1 < κ ≤ 2 the average stays unbiased and consistent, but standard errors from the central limit theorem are invalid ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)).

What the theory guarantees is narrow and exact: E[H] = π(h) for the chosen k, m, L and π0; confidence intervals "with asymptotic guarantees in the number of processors, in contrast with standard MCMC confidence intervals that are justified asymptotically in the number of iterations" ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)); and, under a deadline, an exactly unbiased per-worker estimator (discussed under budgets below). It does not guarantee accuracy at finite R. **Jacob et al.'s own bimodal example returned a 95% interval of [0.799, 0.816] for a quantity whose true value is 0.421.** With proposal variance 1 and π0 = N(10, 1), mean τ was 9 and the 99% quantile 35, so R = 1,000 replicates with k = 50 and m = 500 never saw a chain cross to the far mode before meeting; 9,000 more replicates widened the interval to [−0.353, 1.595] because a few did. With the wider π0 = N(10, 10²), mean τ rose to 769 and the 99% quantile to 9,186, and k = 20,000 with m = 30,000 recovered [0.397, 0.430] ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). The census found the same kind of failure without multimodality. On AR(1) with ρ = 0.9 started from a point mass at 10, with k at the 90% meeting-time quantile and m = 10k, the sample excess kurtosis of H was 687 to 839, and **nominal 95% intervals covered 76.5% of the time with R = 50 and 86.3% with R = 500**, against 97.3% and 96.5% from a stationary start ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). In the discussion, Wilkinson's AR(1) latent-field Gibbs example had a mean coupling time of about 2.6×10³ and a maximum of 67,000 over 1,000 replicates, and Sherlock's 20-dimensional Gaussian needed k beyond the 0.999 quantile of τ before the MSE became acceptable, because the conditional variance of the bias correction grows like (τ − k)³/(m − k)² ([JRSSB discussion](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)).

The authors state the remaining limits plainly. "Coupling pairs of Markov chains does not improve their marginal mixing properties", unfortunate initial distributions "can severely affect the performance", stopping chains before they meet gives "unreliable estimators", and the choice of π0 is an open question ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). The review adds that the meeting-time sample itself can mislead under multimodality, since "the user might then observe a small empirical average of the meeting time, even after many independent runs. Yet the expectation of the meeting time could be much larger" ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). The read paper drew 19 discussion contributions by the census's count. The critiques that matter for users are Wilkinson's, that debiasing corrects burn-in in every replicate rather than eliminating it, so Amdahl's law caps the parallel speed-up at (b + n)/b and makes very large processor counts inefficient, and Robert et al.'s warning that "unbiasedness is not the ultimate goal" ([JRSSB discussion](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)). The reply reconciles the first: the estimators deliver "(2α, T)" gains, double precision in the same time, in most settings, while "(α, T/2)" gains, the same precision in half the time, need a moderate or large starting budget ([JRSSB reply](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)). Wang, Blanchet and Glynn reach a similar verdict: unbiased estimators "typically have superior completion times" but "may not automatically provide substantial savings in overall computational costs" ([Wang, Blanchet & Glynn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01431)). No published procedure detects infinite variance or heavy-tailed meeting times from output, so every warning statistic in the literature and in the census is a heuristic ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)).

## Couplings meet only where the kernel contracts

A coupling has two jobs: to contract the two chains while they are far apart, and to turn a small difference into exact equality. Every scalable construction in the literature does the first with a synchronous coupling (common random numbers, CRN) or a gradient-aligned one, and the second with an occasional maximal coupling; maximal couplings alone meet in one step with probability 1 − TV but do not contract, which is why their meeting times grow exponentially with dimension ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [O'Leary 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01790)). Kernels whose moves only translate differences, or move points along lines between walkers, have nothing to contract with ([census synthesis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/advanced_couplings.md)).

### Reflection-maximal proposals with a shared uniform are the gradient-free workhorse

Three exact primitives cover a NumPy core. Thorisson's rejection algorithm, the "γ-coupling", couples any two distributions that have samplers and normalised log-densities, meets with probability 1 − TV and costs two draws on average, but **its cost variance is 2(1 − TV)/TV**, which diverges as the distributions approach each other; at TV = 0.008 the census measured a variance of 252 draws and a maximum of 1,075 in one call ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/mh_couplings.md)). Gerber and Lee's variant with η < 1 caps the variance at 2η/(1 − η) at the price of a sub-maximal meeting probability, which the census measured as ∫min(ηp, q); at η = 0.9 the maximum fell to 97 draws ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851); [census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/mh_couplings.md)). The reflection-maximal coupling of N(μ1, Σ) and N(μ2, Σ), due to Bou-Rabee et al., is maximal at a deterministic cost of d normals and two uniforms, and meets with probability 2Φ(−‖Σ^{−1/2}(μ1 − μ2)‖/2) ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [O'Leary 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01790)). Categorical distributions have an O(K) maximal coupling, best computed in log space ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [coupled_rejection_sampler.py](https://github.com/AdrienCorenflos/coupled_rejection_sampling/blob/master/coupled_rejection_sampling/coupled_rejection_sampler.py)). Gaussians with unequal covariances have no deterministic-cost maximal coupling; Corenflos and Särkkä's coupled rejection sampler bounds the runtime variance but is sub-maximal and still a preprint ([Corenflos & Särkkä](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09585)).

For Metropolis–Hastings kernels, O'Leary and Wang proved that every coupling of two MH transitions is a coupling of proposals followed by a coupling of accept/reject indicators with the correct conditional acceptance probabilities ([O'Leary & Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00366)). Johnson's construction, a maximal proposal coupling plus one shared uniform, is faithful and needs at most two target evaluations per step: "The chains will stay together after they meet" ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). It is not maximal at the kernel level. O'Leary, Wang and Jacob's P̄C reaches the kernel bound at the same cost and helps when acceptance is low (mean τ of 61 against 74 on their Expo(1) example), while their full-kernel couplings reach it at a random cost with unbounded variance and a fresh target evaluation in every inner loop ([O'Leary, Wang & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08573)). The census reproduced their table but found P̄C slower than Johnson's coupling from about d = 10 on standard Gaussians (309.7 against 259.5 at d = 50), and Corenflos and Särkkä report an implementation bug that reverses one of the paper's high-dimensional comparisons, which no erratum confirms ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/mh_couplings.md); [Corenflos & Särkkä](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09585)). Within Johnson's scheme the choices are ordered: on N(0, I₁₀), mean τ with a shared uniform was **30 for reflection-maximal proposals, 54 for semi-independent, 104 for optimal-transport and 279 for maximal-independent residuals**, and the shared uniform beat independent and antithetic acceptance in every row ([O'Leary 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01790)).

### Without gradients, scalable random-walk couplings stop at a few hundred dimensions

Dimension is where couplings fail. Two independent chains start with ‖X − Y‖² = O(d), while a proposal meeting is likely only once ‖X − Y‖² = O(1/d) at the mixing-optimal scale ℓ/√d, so a coupling that cannot contract the chains is expected to meet in a time exponential in d, and every step in which one chain accepts and the other rejects pushes them Θ(1) apart again ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)). Maximal-independent proposals show the exponential growth, with mean τ of 5.0 at d = 2 and 274 at d = 10 in the census's check, while reflection-maximal proposals grow only polynomially, close to linearly: **2.8, 32.3, 252.8, 629 and 3,565 at d = 1, 10, 50, 100 and 400**, about d^1.3, from independent stationary starts on N(0, I) ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/mh_couplings.md); [Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). The reflection coupling holds the chains at an O(1) distance, close enough for coalescence when d ≤ 100 but unlikely an order of magnitude higher, and it fails on eccentric targets, where it "must sacrifice mixing for contraction" with step sizes that shrink to zero ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)). Papp and Sherlock's GCRN coupling synchronises the gradient-direction components of the two proposals, is provably optimally contractive in their high-dimensional limit and is insensitive to eccentricity; their two-scale scheme switches to reflection-maximal below a squared-distance threshold chosen by a logarithmic grid search. On a 360-dimensional stochastic-volatility posterior, reflection-maximal alone had "effectively null" coalescence probability while two-scale GCRN met ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)). Their conclusion sets the ceiling for a gradient-free package: "it is unclear to us how one could devise similarly scalable couplings of the RWM algorithm without such information" ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)). Preconditioning is not optional either: on a 61-dimensional logistic regression with a diagonal preconditioner only the gradient-corrected reflection coupling was practical, while a full Cholesky preconditioner made plain reflection workable ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)).

Two rules govern how couplings combine. Switching between couplings of the same kernel as a function of the current pair is legal, because each branch preserves the marginals; mixing different kernels with weights that depend on the distance between chains is not, since "it would not be legal to employ P̄₁ when the chains are distant and P̄₂ when they are close" ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). The step size can stay near the marginal optimum ℓ ≈ 2.38 once the target is well preconditioned, although Papp and Sherlock caution that smaller steps may be needed to synchronise acceptances ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)).

### Gradient samplers couple through contraction plus an occasional maximal step

Independence MH is the simplest exact-meeting kernel, because a shared proposal and a shared uniform are already kernel-maximal, and coupled particle independent MH has τ geometric given the initial state with P(τ = 1) ≥ 1/2 ([O'Leary, Wang & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08573); [Middleton et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01781)). MALA proposals share a covariance, so reflection-maximal coupling gives exact meetings directly, as Biswas, Jacob and Vanetti used for convergence diagnostics, but no published theory bounds coupled-MALA meeting times in dimension, and Heng and Jacob's Gaussian study shows them only in a figure, at about 150 for d = 100 as read from its axes ([Biswas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09971); [Heng & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00404)). With both samplers' couplings tuned, Papp and Sherlock found preconditioned RWM 2 to 4 times slower than MALA with a diagonal preconditioner and 7 to 13 times slower with a full one ([Papp & Sherlock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12585)).

HMC has one published recipe for exact meeting. Heng and Jacob share the momentum and the accept/reject uniform between the chains, which contracts them, and with probability γ = 1/20 apply a random-walk MH step with σ = 10⁻³ whose proposals are maximally coupled, which turns very close into equal ([Heng & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00404)). On a 302-dimensional logistic regression, the HMC-optimal setting (ε, L) = (0.03, 10) did not contract, and the pilot-chosen (0.0125, 10) gave a relative inefficiency of 1.05 with k at the 90% quantile and m = 10k, although Xu et al. later reported that these Metropolis-HMC figures are off by about a factor of two because of an error; on a log-Gaussian Cox process, relative inefficiency was 11.00, 5.43 and 2.73 at d = 256, 1,024 and 4,096 ([Heng & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00404); [Xu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/xu21i.html)). Multinomial HMC with maximal or W2 optimal-transport couplings of the trajectory index was more robust to the step size, with relative inefficiency of 1.90 and 0.94 on logistic regression but 3.83 and 5.80 on the Cox process, and Bou-Rabee et al.'s contractive momentum coupling cut mean τ on a Rosenbrock target from 158 to 52 ([Xu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/xu21i.html); [Heng & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00404)). **No coupled NUTS has been published**: Xu et al. list it as future work, and Bou-Rabee and Oberdörster's coupling of NUTS is a mixing-time proof, not an estimator ([Bou-Rabee & Oberdörster](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06978v1)). Unadjusted and kinetic Langevin samplers reach unbiasedness by a different route, randomised multilevel Monte Carlo across step sizes, with O(d^{1/4}ε^{−2}) gradient evaluations claimed by Chada et al. in a preprint reportedly in press at the Annals of Statistics ([Chada et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05025)).

### Gibbs and slice samplers need two-scale couplings in high dimension

Gibbs samplers are coupled one conditional at a time. Maximal couplings of the conditionals produce meetings, CRN contracts but the chains "may come arbitrarily close but will never exactly meet", and maximal couplings alone gave meeting times that grew exponentially with the number of covariates p in shrinkage regression ([Biswas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04798)). Biswas, Bhattacharya, Jacob and Johndrow's two-scale coupling, CRN while a Rao–Blackwellised distance exceeds d₀ = 0.5 and maximal below it, cut meeting times by orders of magnitude and brought a half-t(2) regression with **p = 98,385 covariates to converge in under 1,000 iterations**, at about 60 s per coupled iteration ([Biswas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04798)). Discrete Gibbs samplers work until critical slowing down: single-site Ising updates had meeting times above 10⁶ as θ approached 0.48, and Bayesian variable selection grew linearly in p to 10⁴ and 10⁵ steps ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Component-wise MH-within-Gibbs couples each block separately, and a coordinate that has met can separate again while others differ, so only full-vector equality counts as a meeting ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/mh_couplings.md); [Ceriani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08939)). Slice sampling appears in the coupling literature only as one block of Biswas et al.'s sampler, and no coupled elliptical slice sampler exists. The census found that CRN-coupled elliptical slice sampling contracts to machine precision on a Gaussian-process classification model, and that mixing in a reflection-maximal pCN step produces meetings with median τ of 26, 68, 120 and 909 at d = 10, 50, 100 and 200, but only 2 meetings in 20 within 5,000 iterations at d = 400, in a design that confounds dimension with data size ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/advanced_couplings.md)).

### Ensemble moves cannot be coupled, so the ensemble belongs in the pilot

This is the census's most consequential negative result, because emcee users expect an ensemble. No coupling of affine-invariant or differential-evolution ensemble moves has been published, and the census found structural reasons why none works. A stretch proposal for a walker lies on the line through that walker and its partner, so the two ensembles' proposal laws sit on different lines, are mutually singular, and have a maximal-coupling meeting probability of zero; a walker that has met separates again whenever its partner has not; and because the moves are affine-equivariant, CRN preserves any affine relation between the ensembles, so the deterministic part of the move cannot contract them. For stretch factor a = 2, a jointly accepted update multiplies the expected square of an uncorrelated walker difference by about 1.77 ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/advanced_couplings.md)). The simulations agreed. Over 20,000 sweeps, CRN-coupled ensembles stayed as far apart as independent ones; ensembles started 10⁻⁸ apart drifted apart exponentially, reaching O(1) within 300 sweeps at d = 1; and adding a γ = 1/20 maximally coupled random-walk step on the whole ensemble produced **zero meetings in 50,000 iterations for d = 1, 2, 5 and 10**, while single-chain reflection-maximal RWMH on the same targets met with median τ of 6 to 323 for d = 1 to 50 ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/advanced_couplings.md)). The defensible design runs the ensemble only as an independent pilot that supplies π0 and the preconditioner for coupled single-chain kernels, which matches the pilot-then-freeze practice of the published coupled-MCMC work ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [Xu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/xu21i.html)). The evidence is unpublished, so under the project's rule for new constructions (DECISIONS #1) it stays an experiment until the lead has reviewed it.

### Multimodal, particle, piecewise-deterministic and discrete samplers sit outside a black-box v0.1

Replica exchange couples by sharing swap decisions and maximally coupling the updates within each replica, but a meeting then requires every replica to coincide, so mean τ on the 32 × 32 Ising model first fell and then rose with the number of replicas, with a minimum at 16 ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). The most robust published route for multimodal static targets runs coupled particle independent MH and coupled conditional SMC over a tempered SMC sampler; on a 35-node Gaussian graphical model, 30 runs finished within 72 hours on 16 cores, 19 met at τ = 1 and the largest τ was 1,010, and the authors warn that discarding unfinished runs biases the result ([van den Boom et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.05176)). Coupled conditional particle filters keep mean τ roughly constant when the number of particles grows linearly with the time horizon, backward sampling removes that requirement, and coupled particle marginal MH develops heavy-tailed meeting times as likelihood estimates get noisier ([Jacob, Lindsten & Schön](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1701.02002); [Lee, Singh & Vihola](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.05852); [Middleton et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08691)). Bouncy-particle and Boomerang samplers couple with relative inefficiency of 1.25 and 1.35 on a 16-dimensional logistic regression, but they need event-rate bounds for thinning, and the zig-zag sampler resists coupling altogether ([Corenflos, Sutton & Chopin](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15422v2)). Partition samplers meet reliably only under optimal-transport couplings of their Gibbs conditionals, where label-based couplings failed on about 10% of attempts ([Nguyen, Trippe & Broderick](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.11258)), and the census found no coupling of reversible-jump samplers. All of these need model structure that a black-box log-density does not expose.

### Adaptation cannot be coupled: tune on a pilot, then freeze

Adaptive MCMC breaks both pillars of the estimator: each chain's marginal law is no longer that of a fixed kernel started at π0, and two chains adapting on different histories run different kernels after their states coincide, so meetings do not persist ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/advanced_couplings.md)). The review says that a successful coupling of adaptive transitions "remains elusive", and CoupledHMC.jl's coupled iterator carries the comment "FIXME: Adaptation is not supported" ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851); [CoupledHMC.jl](https://github.com/TuringLang/CoupledHMC.jl)). Every published application tunes on independent preliminary runs and freezes the result: proposal covariances and π0 from pilot moments, HMC step sizes from pilot meeting times, SMC schedules from one adaptive run, and k, m and L from pilot meeting-time quantiles ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [Heng & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00404); [van den Boom et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.05176)). Re-tuning between batches of replicates keeps each replicate conditionally unbiased given its tuning, but the replicates are then neither independent nor identically distributed, and no published treatment exists ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/advanced_couplings.md)).

## Pilot, freeze, couple: the defaults the evidence supports

### One meeting-time quantile sets k, L, m and the burn-in diagnostic

The best-supported default is Atchadé and Jacob's: draw meeting times with L = 1, set k to the 99% quantile of τ − L, then set L = k and m = 10k, because "setting L = k leads to a minor increase of cost per estimator compared to L = 1, and thus the efficiency is typically improved, sometimes drastically"; their figure shows relative inefficiency falling from about 10⁴ to about 1 as k grows ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)). Douc et al. set L to a large quantile, k = L and m = 5k, and CoupledHMC.jl's helper uses the 90% quantile with m a multiple of k ([Douc et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05691); [CoupledHMC.jl analysis.jl](https://github.com/chalk-lab/CoupledHMC.jl/blob/main/src/analysis.jl)). The census's AR(1) check shows why the quantile matters: relative inefficiency was 244 at k = m = 1, 26.4 with k at the 90% quantile and 1.89 at the 99% quantile, both with m = 10k ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Sherlock's 20-dimensional counterexample, where only the 0.999 quantile gave acceptable error, argues for a conservative mode rather than a different default ([JRSSB discussion](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)).

One identity ties tuning to diagnostics. The L-lag bound on total variation satisfies E[max(0, ⌈(τ − L − k)/L⌉)] = Σ_{j≥1} P(τ > k + jL), so once L is large enough that P(τ > k + 2L) ≈ 0, the bound at k equals P(τ − L > k), and "k at the 99% quantile of τ − L" means "an estimated TV bound of 0.01 at k" ([census derivation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/diagnostics_practice.md)). Telescopic can therefore use one quantity, the empirical tail of τ − L, both to choose k and to report burn-in. Tuning must come from an independent pilot: choosing k from the replicates that are averaged breaks exact unbiasedness, for the same reason that Craiu and Meng's coefficients must be estimated leave-one-out ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/theory_estimators.md)). No source gives a rule for the number of replicates R, or for the pilot size needed to estimate a 99% quantile; published runs use 100 to 10,000 replicates, and the bimodal example shows that 1,000 can mislead ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [Biswas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09971)).

### The initial distribution decides whether multimodality is seen

Any π0 gives unbiased estimates, "including point masses, but this choice has an impact on both the computing cost and the variance", and the authors recommend "trying more than one choice of initialization, especially in the setting of multimodal targets" ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Under a geometric drift condition the tail bound P(τ > n) ≤ C′π0(V)κⁿ makes the dependence explicit through the mean of the drift function under π0 ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Practice favours spread-out starts. Biswas et al. initialised regression chains from the prior, phylogenetic chains started from short prior runs because random trees are nearly maximally far apart, Papp and Sherlock note that "the prior is often a suitable initialization, because it tends to be less concentrated than the (target) posterior", and Ryder et al. proposed drawing (X0, Y0) from (p, p′) or (p′, p) with a fair coin, so that both marginals are ½(p + p′) while the chains start in different regions ([Biswas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04798); [Kelly, Ryder & Clarté](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13328); [Papp & Sherlock 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11627); [JRSSB discussion](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)). Different pairs may use different initial distributions and stay unbiased, but the replicates are then not identically distributed, and the reply advises care with intervals ([JRSSB reply](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)). For emcee users the bridge is direct: the final positions of an existing ensemble define a valid empirical π0 ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/api_ux_samplers.md)).

### Coupling bounds and R̂ answer different questions

Biswas, Jacob and Vanetti's bounds, d_TV(π_t, π) ≤ E[max(0, ⌈(τ^(L) − L − t)/L⌉)] and an analogous 1-Wasserstein bound, hold for any coupling and any π0, need no stationarity assumption, and sharpen with L at roughly linear cost ([Biswas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09971)). They are upper bounds for the chosen π0 and coupling; "poor couplings give poor bounds", and "if τ has large variance, the bounds can be dramatically over-confident" ([Statisfaction](https://statisfaction.wordpress.com/2019/06/05/estimating-convergence-of-markov-chains/)). Against exact distances they behave well with a large lag. On AR(1) with L = 50, the estimated TV bound was within 0.006 of the exact TV for t ≥ 10 and never more than 0.5 standard errors below it, and on a six-state chain the exact bound dominated the exact TV at every t, with values at t = 10 of 0.892, 0.223 and 0.141 for L = 1, 5 and 20 against a true 0.0483 ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Craiu and Meng's tighter bound reuses the same meeting times but gains little once L is large ([Craiu & Meng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12662)).

Rank-normalised R̂, effective sample size and the integrated autocorrelation time answer a different question, the variance of time averages under approximate stationarity: Vehtari et al. recommend R̂ < 1.01, at least four chains and ESS above 400, and emcee recommends runs longer than about 50 autocorrelation times ([Vehtari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08008); [emcee autocorrelation tutorial](https://emcee.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/autocorr/)). Neither family certifies the other: in phylogenetics the coupling bound exposed slow convergence of model components that the field's standard ASDSF statistic missed, and Jacob et al. separated poor mixing from poor coupling by computing ESS on long plain runs ([Kelly, Ryder & Clarté](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13328); [Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Because coupled replicates are genuinely independent, telescopic can compute R̂ across the X-chains of different replicates, which emcee's documentation advises against doing across walkers; disagreement between R̂ and the coupling bound is itself a warning ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/diagnostics_practice.md)).

### Warnings must target the tails that fooled every documented case

In every documented failure the point estimate and its standard error looked fine until rare long meetings appeared, so telescopic's warnings must examine tails rather than estimates ([census synthesis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/diagnostics_practice.md)). The candidates the census derived from those cases are pairs censored at the iteration cap; a heavy meeting-time tail, such as q₀.₉₉/q₀.₈ or max/median above about 10 as in Wilkinson's example, or a Hill tail index near 2 or below, where finite variance fails; one replicate contributing more than about a quarter of the sample variance; a non-trivial fraction of replicates with τ − L > k; a TV-bound curve that plateaus or exceeds 1 at t = 0; disagreement between pilot and main meeting times, or between two initial distributions; pairs that stay close without meeting, which in phylogenetics revealed non-overlapping proposals; and relative inefficiency far above 1 ([census synthesis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/diagnostics_practice.md); [Kelly, Ryder & Clarté](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13328)). **None of these thresholds has been calibrated**, so the specification should treat them as configurable defaults and their calibration as a study telescopic owes its users. The R package's handling of the iteration cap is the anti-pattern: it returns `meetingtime = Inf` with no warning, and the returned estimate silently omits the rest of the bias correction ([unbiasedestimator.R](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/master/R/unbiasedestimator.R)).

Reporting conventions are otherwise settled. Estimates are means of R replicates with CLT intervals, histograms show per-bin estimates with ±2 SE boxes that can go negative, meeting times appear as histograms, quantiles and log-scale survival curves, efficiency is inefficiency divided by V∞, and comparisons with "one long run" should state "the number of processors, budget constraints, the desired precision, the choice of k and m" and the burn-in ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [Kelly, Ryder & Clarté](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13328); [JRSSB reply](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170901)).

## Nothing installable exists in any language

**No released, maintained package for unbiased MCMC with couplings exists on PyPI, CRAN or the Julia General registry.** A scan of all 920,853 PyPI project names, the 25,498 CRAN packages and the Julia registry found nothing relevant, and the name `telescopic` was free on PyPI on 2026-10-23 ([PyPI simple index](https://pypi.org/simple/); [CRAN package list](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/available_packages_by_name.html); [Julia Registry.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliaRegistries/General/master/Registry.toml); [PyPI telescopic](https://pypi.org/pypi/telescopic/json)). The reference implementation is Pierre Jacob's GitHub-only R package `unbiasedmcmc` (v0.3.1, GPL ≥ 2, 59 commits from 2017 to September 2026), whose README says "This is not a general-purpose statistical software, but a collection of scripts intended to reproduce figures and tables. Use at your own risk!!"; it has no automated tests, only 18 manual check scripts, and its latest commit fixed a bug in a Pólya–Gamma Gibbs coupling found by Tamás Papp ([unbiasedmcmc README](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/master/README.md); [unbiasedmcmc](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc)).

Every Python port stopped within months. ColCarroll's `couplings` (MIT, 2019) is a library plus an essay whose unbiased-estimator chapters were never published; it declares meetings with `np.isclose`, uses lag-1 weights, silently sets the correction to zero for pairs that never met, and draws from NumPy's global random state ([metropolis_hastings.py](https://github.com/ColCarroll/couplings/blob/master/couplings/metropolis_hastings.py); [couplings essay](https://colcarroll.github.io/couplings/)). `HMC_unbiased` stops when any single coordinate coincides and caps silently at 10,000 iterations, ylefay's JAX port is a course project, and O'Leary's GPL-3 code runs meeting-time experiments without estimators ([HMC_unbiased main.py](https://github.com/Helmuthn/HMC_unbiased/blob/main/HMC_unbiased/main.py); [ylefay](https://github.com/ylefay/unbiased_mcmc_with_couplings); [mh-max-couplings](https://github.com/johnoleary/mh-max-couplings/blob/main/src/mcmc.py)). Julia has CoupledHMC.jl, unlicensed and last changed in 2022, which declares meetings at `atol = 1e-10` and needs AdvancedHMC's master branch, plus two MIT research packages from Corenflos with tests but no registry entry, for PDMP couplings and f-divergence diagnostics ([CoupledHMC.jl](https://github.com/chalk-lab/CoupledHMC.jl); [CoupledPDMPs](https://github.com/AdrienCorenflos/CoupledPDMPs); [ImportanceMCMC](https://github.com/AdrienCorenflos/ImportanceMCMC)).

The general libraries stop at kernels. BlackJAX added `blackjax.mcmc.coupled_hmc` on 2026-09-07 (release 1.7.0), a pair of HMC chains sharing their random inputs, whose docstring says it "does not claim product-target invariance, meeting, unbiased estimators, or an efficiency improvement" ([coupled_hmc.py](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/blob/main/blackjax/mcmc/coupled_hmc.py); [PR #1033](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/pull/1033)). Its maintainers closed the 2021 request for unbiased HMC in March 2026 as "a niche algorithm with no owner", and the replacement issue rates the effort as large and answers "Willing to open a PR?" with "No" ([issue #64](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/issues/64); [issue #826](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/issues/826)). Corenflos's 2021 implementation attempt was closed unmerged after he wrote "I think I got the initial design wrong": the method behaves as a meta-algorithm around kernels and does not fit a step-kernel API ([PR #65](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/pull/65)). TFP's FunMC spinoff has a batched reflection-maximal proposal primitive but is not on PyPI, AdvancedHMC synchronises random numbers across chains, and NumPyro, PyMC, Stan, emcee, zeus, particles, Pigeons and ArviZ offer no couplings, meeting times or estimators ([fun_mc_lib.py](https://github.com/tensorflow/probability/blob/main/spinoffs/fun_mc/fun_mc/fun_mc_lib.py); [AdvancedHMC utilities.jl](https://github.com/TuringLang/AdvancedHMC.jl/blob/main/src/utilities.jl); [census](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/software_landscape.md)).

Uptake outside the methods community is thin, which the census attributes partly to the missing tooling. The JRSSB paper has 97 Crossref and 107 OpenAlex citations, against 597,357 monthly downloads for emcee and 73,110 for dynesty ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/rssb.12336); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/rssb.12336); [pypistats emcee](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/emcee/recent); [pypistats dynesty](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/dynesty/recent)). Jacob's companion site lists applications in phylogenetics, variational autoencoders and variable selection but none in astronomy, physics or ecology, and the only astronomy-facing coupling code is a one-week prototype of coupling from the past ([companion site](https://pierrejacob.quarto.pub/unbiased-mcmc/articles.html); [mcmc_infinity](https://github.com/cjm96/mcmc_infinity)).

Licences constrain reuse. The most complete code is copyleft: `unbiasedmcmc` and `debiasedhmc` are GPL ≥ 2 and O'Leary's and Papp's coupling code is GPL-3, so a line-by-line Python translation would be a derivative work, while the algorithms, equations and pseudocode in the papers carry no code copyright ([unbiasedmcmc DESCRIPTION](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/master/DESCRIPTION); [mh-max-couplings LICENSE](https://github.com/johnoleary/mh-max-couplings/blob/main/LICENSE); [rwmcouplings DESCRIPTION](https://github.com/tamaspapp/rwmcouplings/blob/main/DESCRIPTION)). LlagCouplings, partition-coupling, CoupledPDMPs, ImportanceMCMC and the ColCarroll and ylefay ports are MIT, BlackJAX and FunMC are Apache-2.0, and CoupledHMC.jl, `coupled_rejection_sampling` (whose LICENSE file is empty) and CoupledHalfT have no licence ([LlagCouplings LICENSE](https://github.com/niloyb/LlagCouplings/blob/master/LICENSE); [coupled_rejection_sampling LICENSE](https://github.com/AdrienCorenflos/coupled_rejection_sampling/blob/master/LICENSE); [census](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/software_landscape.md)). An MIT telescopic should be a clean-room implementation from the papers that runs the GPL packages only as external oracles for reference numbers and keeps notices for any MIT or Apache code it adapts. Jacob's repositories since 2022 are MIT, so a relicensing request for specific helpers is plausible but unnecessary ([unbiasedpoisson](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedpoisson); [unbiasedsnis](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedsnis)).

## emcee's workflow transfers; its walkers, seeds and weights do not

emcee 3.1.6, unchanged since April 2024, sets the expectations telescopic's users will bring: a plain `log_prob(x, *args)` returning −∞ outside the support, an `(nwalkers, ndim)` start built as an optimum plus a small jitter, `run_mcmc` or a `sample()` generator, a backend with `get_chain(discard, thin, flat)` and an optional HDF5 file, and a weighted mixture of move objects ([emcee ensemble.py](https://github.com/dfm/emcee/blob/main/src/emcee/ensemble.py); [emcee FAQ](https://emcee.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/faq/)). Its documented weaknesses are as clear. The default stretch move "should not be relied upon (in isolation) in moderate to high dimensions", and above about 50 dimensions its output "can resemble a successful run" while the means and variances are wrong ([Huijser, Goodman & Brewer](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.02230)); it seeds a legacy `RandomState` from global state and warns that setting it can fail silently ([emcee ensemble.py](https://github.com/dfm/emcee/blob/main/src/emcee/ensemble.py); [emcee #313](https://github.com/dfm/emcee/issues/313)); `vectorize=True` silently ignores the `pool`; and user input is checked with `assert` ([emcee ensemble.py](https://github.com/dfm/emcee/blob/main/src/emcee/ensemble.py)). Walkers give the clearest naming lesson. emcee's documentation says Gelman–Rubin must not be computed across walkers "because the chains are not independent!", yet ArviZ's `from_emcee` puts walkers on the `chain` axis, so R̂ is computed across them by default ([emcee autocorrelation tutorial](https://emcee.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/autocorr/); [arviz-base 1.3.1](https://pypi.org/project/arviz-base/1.3.1/)). Users therefore hand-roll stopping loops on the autocorrelation time, and wrappers such as RadVel add Gelman–Rubin across several independent ensembles ([RadVel](https://radvel.readthedocs.io/en/latest/mcmc.html)). Hogg and Foreman-Mackey frame the opportunity: "the only thing a sampling is good for is computing integrals", and a likelihood should return "exactly the same answer" when called again at the same parameters ([Hogg & Foreman-Mackey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06068)). Unbiased estimators are integrals with error bars from independent replicates, and determinism is a hard requirement for coupled chains, because a stochastic log-density can split chains that have met.

dynesty's 3.0 redesign (October 2025) shows how to grow without a tail of keywords: `sample=` accepts either a string or an `InternalSampler` object, iterators return objects rather than tuples, and results expose per-proposal statistics; 3.1.0 added an ETA ([dynesty CHANGELOG](https://github.com/joshspeagle/dynesty/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md); [dynesty.py](https://github.com/joshspeagle/dynesty/blob/master/py/dynesty/dynesty.py)). Its `rstate` is a `numpy.random.Generator` with `SeedSequence` streams spawned per pool task, its checkpoints are atomic pickles that warn on version mismatch and are not meant as an archive, and its `Results` object is immutable, although `summary()` prints instead of returning and one random-state docstring is stale ([dynesty utils.py](https://github.com/joshspeagle/dynesty/blob/master/py/dynesty/utils.py); [dynesty quickstart](https://dynesty.readthedocs.io/en/stable/quickstart.html)). Most relevant is dynesty's advice that, because live-point proposals are serial, users can parallelise "*after* runtime (by merging the runs together)": for independent coupled replicates this is the native mode ([dynesty quickstart](https://dynesty.readthedocs.io/en/stable/quickstart.html)).

Other samplers contribute specific habits. zeus stops when all its callbacks return true and Eryn accepts a `stopping_fn` ([zeus-mcmc 2.5.4](https://pypi.org/project/zeus-mcmc/2.5.4/); [eryn 1.2.6](https://pypi.org/project/eryn/1.2.6/)); CmdStanPy separates a `summary()` DataFrame from a plain-language `diagnose()` ([cmdstanpy 1.3.0](https://pypi.org/project/cmdstanpy/1.3.0/)); NumPyro's `init_to_*` strategies and PyMC's `SamplingError`, which prints the starting values and per-variable log-probabilities, are models for π0 objects and their validation ([numpyro 0.22.0](https://pypi.org/project/numpyro/0.22.0/); [pymc 6.3.2](https://pypi.org/project/pymc/6.3.2/)); Pigeons promises that its output "is invariant to the number of machines and/or threads" through per-chain splittable streams ([Pigeons distributed.md](https://github.com/Julia-Tempering/Pigeons.jl/blob/main/docs/src/distributed.md)); UltraNest re-evaluates stored points before resuming ([ultranest 4.6.3](https://pypi.org/project/ultranest/4.6.3/)); and BlackJAX's pure `init`/`step` functions and explicit `(first, second)` pair arguments are the contract a future JAX backend should match ([blackjax 1.7.1](https://pypi.org/project/blackjax/1.7.1/)). The anti-patterns recur too: global seeding in pocoMC and emcee, logging-only summaries in zeus, constructors with more than 30 arguments in Eryn, and Cobaya's seed, which in parallel runs "only works until to the first proposer covmat update" ([pocomc 1.2.6](https://pypi.org/project/pocomc/1.2.6/); [cobaya 3.6.2](https://pypi.org/project/cobaya/3.6.2/)).

No mainstream output tool handles negative weights. corner computes weighted quantiles by interpolating a cumulative sum of weights, which NumPy documents as "meaningless" when the abscissa is non-increasing; ArviZ uses weights only to resample; and pocoMC's importance weights are non-negative by construction ([corner 2.3.0](https://pypi.org/project/corner/2.3.0/); [numpy.interp](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.interp.html); [arviz-base 1.3.1](https://pypi.org/project/arviz-base/1.3.1/); [pocomc 1.2.6](https://pypi.org/project/pocomc/1.2.6/)). Telescopic must own its signed-measure summaries and plots, and export to ArviZ only the legitimately unweighted pieces, chiefly the X-chains after k, labelled as such ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/api_ux_samplers.md)).

Unbiased MCMC changes the user contract in six ways: users supply a distribution π0 to draw from repeatedly rather than fixed positions; the unit of work is an independent replicate, a coupled pair run until it meets and reaches m; outputs are signed measures whose test functions can be chosen after the run; k and m come from a pilot; diagnostics centre on meeting times and cost; and deadlines need the Glynn–Heidelberger rule ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [census synthesis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/api_ux_samplers.md)). The evidence favours three layers built from the bottom up: pure kernel and coupling building blocks that return an explicit meeting flag and can be tested in isolation, as in the R package's `single_kernel`/`coupled_kernel` pair and BlackJAX's explicit pairs; an emcee-style sampler object with a pilot, explicit k and m, a replicate generator and an append-only backend; and a one-call function whose automatic choices are reported. The following sketch is consistent with these exemplars; it is not a settled design.

```python
import numpy as np
import telescopic as tel
from scipy import stats

pi0 = stats.multivariate_normal(theta_map, 4 * cov_map)    # or tel.init.empirical(emcee_positions)

res = tel.sample(log_prob, pi0, args=(x, y, yerr),          # layer A: one call, reported defaults
                 n_replicates=512, k="auto", m="auto", rng=2026, checkpoint="fit.h5")
print(res.summary())
res.estimate(lambda th: th[..., 0] * th[..., 1])            # post-hoc test function, replicate SE

sampler = tel.CoupledSampler(log_prob, pi0, args=(x, y, yerr),   # layer B: emcee-style object
                             kernel=tel.kernels.RandomWalkMH(cov=cov_pilot),
                             coupling="reflection-maximal", rng=2026)
tau = sampler.meeting_times(n=200)                         # pilot on its own stream namespace
k = int(np.ceil(np.quantile(tau - 1, 0.99)))
res = sampler.run(n_replicates=1000, k=k, m=10 * k, lag=k, max_iter=100 * k)

kern = tel.kernels.rwmh(log_prob, cov_pilot)               # layer C: building blocks
coup = tel.couplings.reflection_maximal(kern)              # step(sx, sy, rng) -> (sx, sy, met)
pair = tel.run_pair(kern, coup, x0, y0, k=k, m=10 * k, lag=k, rng=rng)
```

Naming follows from the walker confusion: "replicate" or "pair" for an independent coupled pair, "chain X" and "chain Y" for its two marginals, and never "walker" unless an ensemble kernel is coupled ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/api_ux_samplers.md)).

## Deadlines bias naive averages; per-worker rules and indexed streams fix them

### Glynn and Heidelberger's rule is the unbiased way to stop on time

Unbiasedness survives parallel execution only if the set of replicates averaged does not depend on their costs. With a budget t, the average of the replicates completed by t has expectation E[H 1(C ≤ t)], biased by −E[H 1(C > t)], and H and its cost C are correlated through τ ([Glynn & Heidelberger 1990](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/1990/GH90.pdf); [Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). Glynn and Heidelberger's fix is exact: average a processor's completed replicates or, if it has completed none, wait for its first one, i.e. average over max(N(t), 1) replicates; completing every replication in progress instead leaves a bias of order 1/t ([Glynn & Heidelberger 1990](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/1990/GH90.pdf)). Pooling across processors is worse, because the pooled "completed by t" average converges to the wrong value as processors are added at fixed t, and no estimator that uses only information from [0, t] is consistent in that limit: "one must be willing to simulate beyond any fixed, finite time t on at least some processors" ([Glynn & Heidelberger 1991](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/1991/GH91a.pdf)). Their 1991 paper also allows only a preselected subset of processors to overrun, warning that the subset "must be preselected" ([Glynn & Heidelberger 1991](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/1991/GH91a.pdf)). Jacob et al. adopt the per-processor estimator H̃(P, t) = P⁻¹ Σ_p H̃_p(t), give central limit theorems as P → ∞ and t → ∞, note that its completion time grows like log P under geometric tails, and used it for 1,858 estimators from 500 processors in 30 minutes ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). The review adds the warning that matters most for an implementation: "running unbiased MCMC on M ≫ C machines and retaining the C estimators that are first completed would introduce a bias, since the estimator is not independent of its cost" ([Atchadé & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06851)).

The census measured how large these biases are, on a coupled random-walk toy with E[C] = 11.05 and corr(H, C) = −0.195 whose exact mean is known. The pooled completed-by-deadline average was biased by **+0.669 (z = +165) at a budget of 1.4 E[C] with 64 lanes**, and by the same amount with 1,024 lanes, the non-vanishing bias Glynn and Heidelberger predict; keeping the first R completed replicates with refilling was biased by +0.645 (z = +221) at R = 1,024 ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)). Index-fixed R, the per-lane Glynn–Heidelberger rule, preselected overrun lanes and pooling after completing every replicate in progress all showed |z| ≤ 1.3. The unbiased rules trade variance against overrun: the complete-in-progress pool had 1.15 to 1.7 times lower standard deviation than the per-lane rule but a longer makespan (36.5 against 25.9 lockstep iterations with 64 lanes), and it is a consistent ratio estimator rather than an exactly unbiased one ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)).

| Policy | Unbiased? | Census result | Overrun past the deadline | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed R by index, wait for all | exactly | no bias detected | until the slowest of the R | default |
| Per worker: mean of completed, or the first if none | exactly | no bias detected | empty workers only | default under a budget |
| Preselected workers may overrun | exactly | no bias detected | preselected empty workers only | hard deadlines |
| Stop starting, finish all in progress, pool | consistent ratio estimator | no bias detected; lowest variance | every worker | low-variance option |
| Pool everything completed by the deadline | biased | +0.669 (z = +165) | none | never |
| First R completed out of more launched | biased | +0.217 (z = +58) with 64 lanes, R = 256 | none | never |
| Interrupted run, completed so far | biased, unplanned deadline | as above | none | flagged partial result |

### Lockstep batches with refill beat pools and vmap for cheap targets

For cheap log-densities, interpreter overhead dominates. One coupled pair at d = 10 cost 38.5 µs per iteration in a lockstep batch of one and 11.7 µs in a plain per-pair loop, but **0.37 µs per pair-iteration in a lockstep batch of 1,000** ([census benchmark](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)). Batches have their own trap: a batch that runs until all of its pairs have met waits for the slowest, wasting a factor E[max C]/E[C] of 2.34 at 64 lanes and 4.95 at 32,768 lanes for the toy, and 4.70 to 10.95 for geometric meeting times with mean 50. Refilling finished lanes from the next replicate index removes most of the waste, with lane utilisation of 0.73 to 0.92 when R was 16 times the batch size and 0.91 to 0.97 at 64 times, and refilling never biases anything by itself, because only the rule that selects which replicates enter the average can ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)). A 2026 GPU application of unbiased MCMC used the same slot refilling ([Ueno & Kijima](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30239)). JAX's `vmap` of a whole-replicate `while_loop` cannot refill: the batched loop continues while any lane is unfinished and still computes finished lanes, so it pays the full waste factor, and a JAX backend needs persistent lanes with an on-device replicate counter instead ([jax loops.py](https://github.com/jax-ml/jax/blob/main/jax/_src/lax/control_flow/loops.py); [census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)).

Process pools suit expensive log-densities. A task costs about 0.2 ms of overhead, so tasks should carry at least 10 ms of work; shipping an 8 MB closure with every task cost 17.7 ms per task against 0.28 ms with a target cached by a worker initializer, the pattern dynesty's own pool uses; and leaving BLAS threads unpinned in four worker processes made a matrix workload 33 to 41 times slower on the test VM, a magnitude specific to that machine in a direction that is not, which is why emcee's tutorial sets `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` ([census benchmark](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md); [dynesty pool.py](https://github.com/joshspeagle/dynesty/blob/master/py/dynesty/pool.py); [emcee parallel tutorial](https://emcee.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/parallel/)). Python 3.14 made forkserver the default start method on Linux, so lambdas and functions defined in a REPL fail in `ProcessPoolExecutor` unless a dill-based pool such as schwimmbad's is used ([What's New in Python 3.14](https://docs.python.org/3/whatsnew/3.14.html); [concurrent.futures](https://docs.python.org/3/library/concurrent.futures.html); [schwimmbad](https://github.com/adrn/schwimmbad)).

### Random streams keyed by replicate index make results independent of scheduling

Reproducibility needs one random stream per replicate, addressed by its index. NumPy's `SeedSequence(root, spawn_key=(i,))` reproduces the i-th spawned child directly, so any replicate can be regenerated alone, and SPEC 7 standardises a single `rng=` keyword normalised with `np.random.default_rng` ([NumPy parallel random generation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/parallel.html); [SPEC 7](https://scientific-python.org/specs/spec-0007/); [census check](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)). NumPy warns that PCG64 has statistical weaknesses in massively parallel use and recommends PCG64DXSM or Philox there, which matters because cheap targets reach 10⁵ to 10⁶ replicates ([NumPy PCG64DXSM](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/upgrading-pcg64.html)). Per-replicate generators are expensive in a lockstep batch if drawn every step, at 7.8 times the cost of one batch generator with 1,000 lanes, but cheap with blocks pre-drawn every 64 steps, at 1.4 times; block pre-drawing requires a fixed number of random draws per coupled step, which reflection-maximal coupling provides (d normals and two uniforms) and rejection couplings do not ([census benchmark](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md); [census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/mh_couplings.md)). With such streams the census obtained bitwise-identical per-replicate results on one and four workers; only the order of summation differed, which changed the last digits of the total unless the sum ran in index order or used `math.fsum`. The same point evaluated in a batched call and alone also differed bitwise in 8 to 25 of 38 cases for d ≥ 7, so after a meeting Y must be set by copying X and its cached log-density, never by recomputing it ([census check](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)).

### Memory, storage and interrupts follow from the replicate as the unit

Streaming estimators cost dim(h) floats per replicate, whereas stored signed-measure atoms cost about (m − k + 1 + max(0, τ − k − L)) × d floats: about 0.72 MB per replicate at d = 100, k = 100 and m = 1,000, or 7 GB for 10⁴ replicates ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)). That argues for per-replicate estimates by default, atoms from index k on request, and full chains only for debugging; the R package already separates an online estimator that "does not record the trajectories" from stored chains ([unbiasedestimator.R](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/blob/master/R/unbiasedestimator.R)). Because each replicate is a deterministic function of the root seed and its index, a replicate-level checkpoint resumes exactly: completed replicates are appended to an HDF5 file written by one process or to a Zarr store with per-worker shards, a manifest records the root entropy, configuration and versions, and a resume reruns only the missing indices ([h5py](https://docs.h5py.org/en/stable/high/dataset.html); [zarr](https://zarr.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user-guide/performance/); [census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/parallel_engineering.md)). A Ctrl-C is a budget run with an unplanned deadline, so interrupts need the same care: workers should ignore SIGINT as schwimmbad's do, the parent should cancel pending work, and the result should carry an "interrupted" status and no estimate unless a valid rule applies; CPython's process pool has a known issue in which workers keep draining the queue after an interrupt ([schwimmbad multiprocessing.py](https://github.com/adrn/schwimmbad/blob/main/src/schwimmbad/multiprocessing.py); [CPython gh-125886](https://github.com/python/cpython/issues/125886)).

## Index bugs hide below Monte Carlo noise, so validation must be exact

### Kernels: oracle draws, exact marginals and meeting-frequency tests

A coupled kernel is correct only if each coordinate follows exactly the uncoupled transition law from every pair of states, the diagonal is absorbing, and a maximal coupling meets with probability 1 − TV ([Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625)). The R reference package has no automated tests, only scripts that print moments and overlay histograms ([unbiasedmcmc inst/check](https://github.com/pierrejacob/unbiasedmcmc/tree/master/inst/check)). BlackJAX's 1,556-line test file for its coupled HMC is the model to copy: each marginal of the coupled step is compared draw for draw with an independent NumPy oracle at `atol=1e-12`, the second marginal's innovation is rebuilt "entirely outside the module", uniforms are chosen so that both the accept and reject branches run, heterogeneous parameters expose leaks between the pair, and faithfulness is asserted with exact equality ([test_coupled_hmc.py](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/blob/main/tests/mcmc/test_coupled_hmc.py)). Statistical tests then need the right design. The census found exact one-sample tests, a binomial test of the probability of staying put plus a KS test of moved values against the exact continuous part, about ten times more sample-efficient than two-sample tests: against a 5% proposal-scale bug at n = 10⁵ they had power 1.00, against 0.74 for two-sample KS and 0.54 for `anderson_ksamp` ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Most important, **a meeting-frequency test caught a bug that every marginal test missed**: a reflection-maximal implementation that recomputed Y on the meeting event produced bitwise-equal pairs 0.23% of the time against an exact meeting probability of 94.0%, while its marginals were perfect, and a binomial test against 2Φ(−Δ/2) flags it at once ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Exact coupled transition matrices on four- to six-state targets check marginals and faithfulness to machine precision, and Gandy and Scott's sequential invariance tests keep false rejections under control at α = 10⁻⁵ with seven stages, where Geweke's test rejected correct samplers at rates of 0.31 to 0.52 at a nominal 0.01; they exist only in Julia and R and need about 60 lines of Python ([Gandy & Scott](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06465); [MCMCTesting.jl](https://github.com/Red-Portal/MCMCTesting.jl/blob/main/docs/src/general.md); [mcunit](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/mcunit/html/expect_mcmc.html)).

### Estimators: algebra, finite-state expectations and linear SBC

Estimator bugs are nearly invisible to statistical tests because the expected differences telescope. In the census's exact finite-state calculation, using the weight (ℓ − k + 1) instead of (ℓ − k) biased the mean by +0.027 with sd(H) = 11.1, so **detecting it at 5σ needs about 4.2 million replicates**; dropping the min(1, ·) cap or dropping the correction altogether needed about 1.1 and 1.4 million ([census computation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Two runs of 20,000 replicates gave z = +2.75 and +2.93 by chance for bugs whose true z was about 0.3, so an isolated |z| near 3 is evidence of nothing. Correctness therefore has to come from deterministic tests: property-based tests on synthetic coupled paths checking that eq. (2.1) equals the definitional average, that weights sum to one, that τ ≤ k + 1 gives a zero correction, that m = k gives H_k, that lag 1 reduces correctly and that the cost formula holds; exact expectations of H_{k:m} on small chains from an off-diagonal recursion; and a "perfect kernel" test in which P(x, ·) = π, so a CRN coupling meets at τ = 2 ([census analysis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Statistical tests must allow for heavy tails: tolerances of at least five standard errors, sequential escalation to four times R with a fresh seed rather than reruns, and coverage tests only in a slow tier and only from stationary starts ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)).

Exact unbiasedness at any k and m gives one more check that the census derived and calls linear SBC: for any φ(θ, y), E[H_φ(y) − φ(θ̃, y)] = 0 when θ̃ is drawn from the prior and y from the model, at any run length. With the log-likelihood as φ, it caught a sampler that targeted the prior at z = −162, while θ and θ² gave −0.11 and +0.71 ([census simulation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). This matches Modrák et al.'s finding that parameter-only SBC "could never detect large classes of problems including when the posterior is equal to the prior" and their advice to use the joint likelihood as a test quantity ([Modrák et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02383)). The derivation has been verified on one conjugate model only, so it should ship both as a test and as a user-facing validation tool, labelled as a census method.

### Benchmarks with exact answers, and literature numbers to reproduce

Mature samplers mostly assert moments within hand-picked tolerances under fixed seeds: NumPyro seeds every test with 0 and skips flaky ones, BlackJAX rotates its seed daily, PyMC runs KS tests at α = 10⁻³, Stan keeps gold values, and none of BlackJAX, NumPyro or PyMC uses Hypothesis ([NumPyro conftest](https://github.com/pyro-ppl/numpyro/blob/master/test/conftest.py); [BlackJAX fixtures](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax/blob/main/tests/fixtures.py); [PyMC sampler fixtures](https://github.com/pymc-devs/pymc/blob/main/tests/sampler_fixtures.py); [Stan performance tests](https://github.com/stan-dev/performance-tests-cmdstan/blob/master/runPerformanceTests.py)). dynesty's tolerance of four times the run's own reported error is closer to what unbiased estimators allow, since every run carries its own standard error ([dynesty test_gau.py](https://github.com/joshspeagle/dynesty/blob/master/tests/test_gau.py)). The fast tier should use targets with exact answers: finite-state chains with exact meeting-time distributions, TV and E[H]; AR(1) with ρ = 0.9 from a point mass, with exact TV, an exact one-step meeting probability and asymptotic variance 19; equal-covariance Gaussian couplings with exact 1 − TV; and conjugate models for linear SBC ([census synthesis](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Slower tiers can reproduce published numbers with known conventions: O'Leary's mean τ of 30 ± 0.8 for reflection-maximal RWM at d = 10 (no lag), O'Leary, Wang and Jacob's 74 and 61 on the Expo(1) target, the bimodal mixture's mean τ of 20 and 99% quantile of 105, the baseball Gibbs sampler's relative inefficiency of 1.004 at k = 3 and m = 30 against a proven burn-in bound of 140 iterations, the banana target with exact moments E = (1, 1.5) and Var = (0.5, 2.55), and German-credit HMC ([O'Leary 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01790); [O'Leary, Wang & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08573); [Jacob et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625); [Heng & Jacob](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00404); [census derivation](../research_notes/Unbiased%20MCMC%20with%20couplings%20landscape/validation_benchmarks.md)). Every comparison must match the lag, the cost unit and the initialisation, which differ across papers. Performance reports should give meeting-time quantiles, E[cost] in evaluations and seconds, Var(H) with a bootstrap interval, and relative inefficiency against exact asymptotic variances where they exist; plain scripts suit the statistical benchmarks, and asv `track_` functions suit a few headline numbers tracked per commit ([asv](https://asv.readthedocs.io/en/stable/writing_benchmarks.html)).

## Specification lessons: exact flags, honest budgets, layered API

### Scope: one gradient-free default, published options, experiments on the side

The evidence supports a v0.1 whose default kernel is random-walk Metropolis with reflection-maximal proposals and a shared uniform, a proposal covariance estimated in a pilot and then frozen, and a scale near 2.38/√d. Published options that cost little to add are independence MH with a pilot-fitted proposal; O'Leary, Wang and Jacob's P̄C for low-acceptance kernels; MALA with reflection-maximal proposals; two-scale GCRN and gradient-corrected reflection for users who supply gradients; Heng and Jacob's HMC mixture with (ε, L) chosen from pilot meeting times; Gibbs updates with user-supplied conditionals and built-in maximal couplings for Normal, Gamma, inverse-Gamma, Beta and categorical families plus a two-scale CRN switch; and generic rejection and categorical couplings with the Gerber–Lee η exposed. Mixtures of kernels must draw their component with a coin shared by both chains and independent of their states. Coupled ensembles, coupled elliptical slice sampling with pCN, coupled NUTS, surrogate-gradient GCRN and re-tuning between replicate batches belong in `notes/experiments/` under DECISIONS #1; particle MCMC, PDMPs, reversible jump, partition samplers and multilevel Langevin are out of scope for a black-box v0.1. The documentation should state the reach plainly: without gradients, meeting times grow roughly linearly up to a few hundred well-preconditioned dimensions, and eccentric targets defeat the default.

### Estimator and meeting contract

Implement H^{(L)}_{k:m} with the floor/ceil weights, use m for the last index, adopt the review's meeting time τ = inf{t ≥ L : X_t = Y_{t−L}}, and record the convention in every result. Every coupling returns an explicit `met` flag that is true only when the proposals coincide and both chains accept; on `met` the implementation copies X, its log-density and its gradient into Y, and from then on advances a single chain. Exact equality is asserted in a debug mode and in tests and is never approximated. A NaN log-density raises an error naming the offending point, −∞ is a rejection, and the log-density must be deterministic. Pairs that reach `max_iter` are right-censored: flagged, counted, excluded from any estimate presented as unbiased and reported prominently. Each replicate stores its MCMC average and its bias correction separately, together with τ, cost in kernel units and in evaluations, and wall time. Variances and covariances use exact U-statistics over replicates; quantiles come from a monotonised pooled CDF labelled consistent rather than unbiased, with replicate-bootstrap intervals labelled heuristic; robust aggregators, if offered, carry the label "robust, not unbiased".

### Execution, randomness and storage

Fixed R by index is the default. A `Budget` in seconds or in lockstep iterations defaults to the per-worker Glynn–Heidelberger rule, offers preselected overrun workers for hard deadlines and complete-in-progress pooling for lower variance, and never offers first-R-completed or completed-by-deadline averages. Three executors share one contract: a lockstep batch with refill and active-set compaction for vectorised log-densities, a pool executor built on `submit`/`as_completed` for expensive ones, with `.map`-only pools accepted in waves of at least 16 replicates per worker, and a serial executor. Randomness flows from `rng=` to a root `SeedSequence`, with one PCG64DXSM stream per replicate keyed by (namespace, index) so that pilot and main runs never collide, block pre-drawing in lockstep, and reductions in index order; the test suite asserts per-index equality across serial, batched and pooled execution. Workers receive the target once through an initializer, pin BLAS to one thread and ignore SIGINT. Storage defaults to per-replicate estimates, with atoms from index k and full chains as options, in an append-only HDF5 or Zarr store with a JSON manifest, so that a resume reruns only missing indices and an interrupted run returns a flagged partial result.

### Diagnostics and defaults

The pilot draws about 100 to 200 pairs at L = 1 on its own stream namespace, sets k to the rounded-up 99% quantile of τ − L, then L = k and m = 10k, and prints the estimated efficiency loss 1 + (k + E[τ])/(m − k). A conservative mode uses the 0.999 quantile, and a parallel-cheap mode trades efficiency for more replicates. Every result reports the estimate, standard error, interval and R; k, m, L, π0 and the coupling; the median, 90% and 99% quantiles and maximum of τ, with the censored count; mean cost; the fraction of replicates with a non-zero correction; relative inefficiency against an autocorrelation-based MCMC baseline when one is available; R̂ across the replicates' X-chains; and the warning flags listed above. Default plots are the meeting-time histogram with a log-scale survival curve, the L-lag TV bound against iteration with k marked, signed marginal histograms with ±2 SE boxes and an explanation of negative bins, and distance traces for a few pairs. UPAVE is a natural optional efficiency report.

## Conclusion

The census inverts the usual risk profile of a methods package. The mathematics is finished and the formulas are fixed to the index, so the hard part of telescopic is the layer that BlackJAX's maintainers declined to own: meeting detection, lags, censoring, deadlines, random streams and estimators wrapped around ordinary kernels. That layer is also where errors are statistically invisible, since a one-index weight bug needs millions of replicates to surface and a floating-point meeting bug passes every marginal test. Telescopic's correctness will therefore be established mainly by deterministic and exact tests, with statistical tests as a backstop, and its value to users by turning the failure modes the literature documents (heavy tails, a narrow π0, completion-biased deadlines, censored pairs) into explicit outputs rather than better-looking intervals.

The emcee mental model breaks in one specific place, the ensemble. Because ensembles cannot be coupled, telescopic's emcee-like experience has to be built from replicates of single-chain kernels, with the ensemble demoted to the pilot that sets π0 and the preconditioner, and its gradient-free reach stated honestly as a few hundred well-conditioned dimensions. Three questions remain that no source answers and that telescopic must settle with its own evidence: how many replicates make an interval trustworthy, which warning thresholds separate safe runs from misleading ones, and how to give valid intervals for quantiles of a signed measure. Answering them, together with the first coupling-based analyses of astronomy problems, is the research contribution telescopic can make beyond the software itself.
