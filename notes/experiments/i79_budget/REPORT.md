# Budget mode: report (issue #79)

**Verdict under the registered rules: budget mode is unbiased and its intervals cover, but it costs more than 25% more than fixed R at a budget of 10 mean costs per lane on two of the three targets. The evidence therefore recommends not building it now.**

- Rules U (unbiasedness), C (coverage) and P (the positive control) hold.
- Rule E (efficiency) fails in 3 of its 6 tests.

Josh rules on this evidence (DECISIONS #69). Nothing in `src/` changed.

## What was run

**Estimator.** It is GH91's $\bar\mu_1$, with a lane as the processor, exactly as #79 proposes and as `PREREGISTRATION.md` (commit `e534ab7`) states.
- Lane $g$ of $G = 32$ runs the replicate indices $g, g + G, \dots$ with a budget of $B$ kernel steps, counted by `Replicates.cost`.
- A lane keeps the replicates it completed within $B$ and abandons the one in progress. A lane that completed none finishes its first.
- The run's estimate is the equal-weight mean of the lane means, and its interval is the $t$ interval over the 32 lanes.

**Unbiasedness, checked before running.** The argument holds on inspection.
- The event $\{N = n\}$ is symmetric in a lane's first $n$ independent pairs $(H, C)$, which gives $\mathbb E[\tilde H] = \mathbb E[H]$ (GH90, Corollary 7).
- Its premises are R-EXEC-2, and a cost that depends on the replicate alone; both hold in the engine.
- `check_budget.out` confirms the rule exactly, in rational arithmetic, on a discrete law where the average of the completed replicates alone is biased by up to 0.9.

**Prototype.** `budget.py` uses `telescopic.engine.run` unchanged, so every chain's marginal is that of the package's kernel. `check_budget.out` shows it gives the same counts and lane estimates, to 1e-12, as a driver that runs each lane one replicate at a time and abandons at the budget through `max_iter`.

**Runs.** These are in `run.out`, at commit `b406202`.
- Machine: Linux x86_64, Intel Xeon at 2.10 GHz, 4 logical processors.
- Processes: 4, with OMP, OpenBLAS and MKL threads set to 1.
- Versions: Python 3.13.18, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1, telescopic 0.1.0.dev0.
- Cost: 0.98 processor hours, and a wall time of 1,067 s.

**Targets.**
- A Gaussian in d = 5, with RWMH started 3 standard deviations away.
- The banana, with HMC started on the ridge.
- The bimodal mixture, with RWMH started from N(10, 10^2).

Each runs at budgets of b = 1, 3 and 10 times the pilot's mean cost. The fixed-R arm matches the budget arm's mean cost per run to within 1%.

**Scoring.** The scores are in `score.out`. The cell records in `runs/*.npz` are regenerated by `run.py`, which is seeded, and are not committed; `runs/plans.json` is.

## Results of the rules

**U, unbiasedness: holds.** 18 of 18 tests pass, the largest at |z| = 2.89 (the Gaussian, b = 10, x^2). The fixed-R control also passes 18 of 18, with z = -2.27 in the same cell.

**C, coverage: holds.** 18 of 18 tests pass. Where coverage is above 0.95, on the mixture at b = 1 and 3, it is so in both arms.

**E, efficiency: fails.** At b = 10, 3 of 6 tests pass: the Gaussian's 1.21 and 1.14, and the banana's x at 1.245. Three fail:
- the banana's x^2, at 1.35 [1.12, 1.62];
- the mixture's x, at 1.74 [1.44, 2.09];
- the mixture's x^2, at 1.37 [1.20, 1.57].

Both of the mixture's intervals lie wholly above 1.25.

**P, power: holds.** The pooled average of the replicates completed within the budget is biased by:
- +2.09 sd (z = +66) on the Gaussian at b = 1;
- +2.59 sd (z = +82) on the mixture at b = 1;
- +0.20 sd (z = +6.4) on the Gaussian at b = 10.

On the banana it reaches only z = +4.5 (b = 1).

**D, secondary: the per-replicate meeting-time diagnostics are biased.** The mean meeting time pooled over the kept replicates is low in 8 of 9 cells, by up to 24%: on the mixture at b = 3, it is 40.96 against 53.68, at z = -43.5. The estimate weighted by lane agrees with the reference in all 9 cells, with |z| at most 1.3.

In the ninth cell, the mixture at b = 1, every lane keeps exactly one replicate, since B = 265 is less than two costs of at least m = 200. There the two estimates coincide.

#79's sentence "meeting-time diagnostics stay per replicate" is therefore wrong. A build would have to weight every meeting-time diagnostic by lane: the TV and W1 bounds, the late fraction and the tail index.

**Makespan.** A budget bounds the wall time only when each lane fits several replicates.
- At b = 1, 20% to 38% of the lanes complete nothing and overrun. The longest lane averages about 2.8 to 3.0 times B, and reaches 8.7 times B.
- At b = 3, the longest lane averages 1.06 to 1.18 B.
- At b = 10, it averages 1.00 B.

## What surprised me

**The efficiency cost is larger than registered, and it does not fall steadily with b.** I predicted ρ of 1.15 to 1.4 at b = 3, from the charged but unused part of the budget, $(1 + \mathrm{cv}^2)/(2b)$. The measured values are 1.5 to 2.7.

A post hoc split, which was not registered and is printed in `score.out`, separates two parts:
- **The counts.** $\mathbb E[1/\tilde N]\,\mathbb E[\text{charged}]/\mathbb E[C]$ gives 1.28 to 1.35 at b = 3 and 1.08 at b = 10, as predicted.
- **The rest, a variance factor.** $\operatorname{Var}(\tilde H)/(\operatorname{Var}(H)\,\mathbb E[1/\tilde N])$ is 1.1 to 2.4 at b = 3, 1.05 to 1.7 at b = 10, and near 1 at b = 1.

The cause is the weighting. The equal-weight lane mean gives each replicate the weight $1/(G\tilde N_g)$, where fixed R gives every replicate $1/R$. A replicate with a long meeting time, whose $H$ is also the most extreme, takes more of its lane's budget. It therefore sits in a lane with a small $\tilde N$, and gets a large weight.

- The effect is largest at b = 3, where $\tilde N$ varies most (1 to 5).
- It vanishes at b = 1, where $\tilde N$ is almost always 1.
- On the heavy-tailed mixture, it doubles the variance.

The variance factor rests on per-run variances of a heavy-tailed $H$, and has a wide Monte Carlo error; the bootstrap intervals for ρ itself do not depend on it. GH90's exactness holds, but the weighting that buys it is what costs the efficiency.

**The positive control is weak on the banana.** There, H correlates little with cost (about -0.04 in scoping), so keeping only the replicates completed within the budget barely biases it. The Gaussian and the mixture show the bias the design guards against, at z of 66 and 82.

**A scoring bug was fixed before the verdict.** The first version of `score.py` required P in every cell, where the pre-registration requires it in at least one. The committed version follows the pre-registration.

## What would change the verdict

The registered verdict stands as above. These options were not tested here:

- **GH91's $\bar\mu_2$,** in which only preselected lanes may overrun. It shortens the makespan at small b, but does not change the $1/\tilde N$ weighting.
- **A budget large enough that $\tilde N$ hardly varies.** It brings ρ toward the count factor, which is already 1.08 at b = 10. But the mixture's variance factor of 1.7 shows that a heavy-tailed H needs more.
- **An interrupted fixed-R run, completed afterwards** (DECISIONS #20), which the package already offers. It keeps the weights at $1/R$ and stays exactly unbiased, at the price of an unbounded wall time.
