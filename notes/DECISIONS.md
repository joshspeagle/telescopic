# Decisions

Decisions taken with Josh, or after the specification was written. Design decisions
taken while writing the specification are in its decision table
(`docs/design/spec.md`). Outcomes: **adopted**, **changed** (cite the commit),
**deferred**, **revisit**.

| # | Date | Topic | Decision | Reason | Alternatives | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-23 | Scope of v0.1 | Published coupling constructions only. New ones (e.g. a coupled ensemble sampler, coupled NUTS) are prototyped and validated in `notes/experiments/` and promoted only after Josh has seen the evidence (Josh) | keeps v0.1 on proven ground and potential papers under Josh's control | new methods as first-class v0.1 features; no new methods at all | adopted |
| 2 | 2026-10-23 | Process | One working branch (`claude/coupled-mcmc-package-btpupz`). Each milestone is a reviewed unit: tests, `/simplify`, `/code-review` at an effort scaled to its size, fixes, a log entry, then commit and push. No GitHub issues or pull requests (Josh) | less overhead for an autonomous first draft; the same review gates as a pull request | GSPICE-style tracking issues and a draft pull request per milestone | adopted |
| 3 | 2026-10-23 | Review budget | Consecutive milestones may form one review unit: one `/simplify` round and one `/code-review` on their combined diff, sized by the table in `CLAUDE.md`. The reuse and simplification angles may share one reviewer. M0 to M3 get one `/code-review` pass | about 14M tokens must cover nine more milestones; a reviewer's cost is mostly fixed (reading the spec, `CLAUDE.md` and the context), so fewer, larger units buy the same scrutiny for less | one unit per milestone with four reviewers each (DECISIONS #2 as first applied) | adopted |
| 4 | 2026-10-23 | Sampler settings | `max_iter` is fixed by a sampler's first `run`, like `k`, `m`, `lag` and `thin` (spec §8.1) | a `Result` has one cap, which its censoring and its warning name; replicates run under different caps would mix two censoring rules | record the cap per replicate | adopted |
| 5 | 2026-10-23 | Tuning interface | `Kernel.tune(target, initial, rng=None, *, pilot=None)`: `pilot` takes adaptive Metropolis chains already run, so a `Mixture` tunes its components on one pilot (spec §5.1) | a pilot costs 4 max(2000, 200d) evaluations, and every component would learn the same mean and covariance from it | `**options` as the spec first wrote; one pilot per component | adopted |
| 6 | 2026-10-23 | Multimodality warning | The tuning chains disagree when $\hat R > \sqrt 2$ in some coordinate (spec §5.3) | the spec's test, chain means spreading more widely than the chains, is between-chain variance above within-chain variance, which is $\hat R^2 > 2 - 1/n$; a convergence threshold such as 1.1 judges how well the pilot mixed, which this warning is not about | $\hat R > 1.1$ or $1.01$, the convergence rules for MCMC output | adopted; M9 measures its false-alarm rate |
| 7 | 2026-10-23 | Tuning pilot | The 4 adaptive Metropolis chains share one running mean and covariance (step $1/(t+1)$), started from the variances of 100 draws from $\pi_0$; each chain keeps its own global scale (step $(t+1)^{-0.6}$). A tuned random walk uses $2.38/\sqrt d$ (spec §5.3) | chains adapting alone at rate $t^{-0.6}$ did not converge for $d \ge 10$ ($\hat R$ 2 to 3 on $\mathcal N(0, I_{10})$), so $k$ came out 5 to 50 times too large, and at $d = 20$ the pilot pairs never met. Measured $k$ (100 pairs, 3 seeds, `notes/reviews/m4-m5.md`): median at most 1.3 times the exact covariance's for $d \le 20$, including variances from $10^{-4}$ to $10^2$, and 4.4 times at $d = 50$ | Stan's windowed re-estimation (never met on the badly scaled target at $d = 20$); an identity start (2 to 20 times the exact $k$ there, 15 to 33 times at $d = 50$); the adapted scale (median $k$ 1.1 to 1.2 times larger at $d = 20$) | adopted; M9 measures larger $d$ |
| 8 | 2026-10-23 | Sampler runs | `run(thin=None, max_iter=None)`: on the first run `None` means 1 and no cap; on later runs `None`, like `"auto"`, keeps the first run's value, and any other change raises. Settings are validated before the memory estimate and kept only once the first run succeeds. Each run ends by emitting the warnings of spec §7.4 for the cumulative result, except slow pairs and disagreeing tuning chains, emitted as they happen (spec §8.1) | with defaults of 1 and `None`, an explicit `thin=1` after `thin=2` was taken for "unchanged"; spec §6.3 requires a warning for censored pairs, and implementation plan §7 wants warnings by default | the settings in the constructor and `run(n)` alone (altitude review); warnings only from `check()` | adopted |
| 9 | 2026-10-23 | Merging results | `Result.merge` refuses results with different seeds | a replicate index identifies a replicate only under one root seed, and the merged result records one seed | allow it and record no seed | adopted |
| 10 | 2026-10-23 | Kernel contract | `step` returns each chain's acceptance probability instead of an accepted flag (spec §5.1) | dual averaging adapts to the mean acceptance probability (Hoffman & Gelman 2014), and four chains' flags would make it noisier; a flag is visible from the state anyway | keep flags and adapt on them; a separate method for tuning | adopted |
| 11 | 2026-10-23 | Gradient kernels outside the support | MALA and HMC refuse initial states with $\log\pi = -\infty$ (spec §4.2) | the proposal starts from the gradient there, which user code often makes NaN; a refusal names the point and the fix, where a rejection would leave the chain stuck | propose without drift from such states; reject and warn | changed by #14 |
| 12 | 2026-10-23 | MALA's coupling | MALA has no `coupling` parameter: the reflection-maximal coupling is its only one (spec §5.4) | the specification offered no other value, and a parameter with one value is a no-op | a rejection maximal option for comparison, as `RandomWalk` has | adopted |
| 13 | 2026-10-23 | Dual averaging | Hoffman & Gelman's constants ($\gamma = 0.05$, $t_0 = 10$, $\kappa = 0.75$, $\mu = \log 10\epsilon_0$), 1000 iterations on 4 chains started from the RWMH pilot's last states, on their mean acceptance probability; $\epsilon_0 = 1.65\,d^{-1/6}$ for MALA and $d^{-1/4}$ for HMC; HMC adapts its trajectories without the meeting step (spec §5.4, §5.5) | published defaults; the pilot's last states are near stationarity, so the adaptation is not spent on a burn-in; over 5 seeds on a 5-dimensional Gaussian with condition number 100, the stationary acceptance was 0.572 to 0.608 for MALA (target 0.574) and 0.802 to 0.811 for HMC (target 0.8) | Stan's search for $\epsilon_0$; adaptation from draws of $\pi_0$ | adopted |
| 14 | 2026-10-23 | Gradient kernels outside the support | States with $\log\pi = -\infty$ carry a gradient of 0, so MALA and HMC propose from them without drift and accept any proposal of finite density (spec §4.1, §4.2) | the refusal of #11 fired inside the engine at the first replicate that drew such a start, so a run failed or not by its seed and $R$: on a half-normal target from $\mathcal N((3, 0), I)$, where 0.135% of draws fall outside, `sample` raised in 8 of 8 seeds with MALA and 4 of 4 with HMC, losing every finished replicate. With the zero gradient the 8 MALA runs completed; over 40 more seeds the estimates of $E[x_0]$ had combined $z = -1.26$ ($-0.73$ from a truncated $\pi_0$); HMC pairs started outside met in 200 of 200 (altitude review of M6, `notes/reviews/m6.md`). $\pi$ has no mass outside the support, so the kernels stay $\pi$-invariant | refuse such starts (#11); reject every proposal from them, which leaves the chains stuck | adopted |
| 15 | 2026-10-23 | Kernels without a log-density | `Kernel.needs_log_prob`: true by default, false for `Gibbs`, true for a `Mixture` with a component that needs it. The sampler and `init_state` refuse `log_prob=None` when it is true (spec §4.1, §5.1, §5.7) | the sampler's `isinstance(kernel, Gibbs)` refused a mixture of Gibbs kernels (a random-scan sampler), and nothing below the sampler checked: `engine.run(Target(None), RandomWalk(I), ...)` ran on $\log\pi \equiv 0$ and estimated $E[x_0]$ as 40.8 (altitude review of M6) | a type check that looks into mixtures; no check below the sampler | adopted |
| 16 | 2026-10-23 | Gibbs validation | `coupling="crn"` refuses an `eta` other than 1 (spec §5.7) | the common-random-number coupling never uses the maximal coupling, so another `eta` is a mistake that would pass silently; `threshold` outside `"two-scale"` is refused for the same reason | ignore `eta` there; a default of `None` | adopted |
| 17 | 2026-10-23 | HMC trajectory length | The default `n_steps` is $\lceil 1/\epsilon\rceil$, at most 1,000; a given `n_steps` is kept (spec §5.5) | the default follows the step size through dual averaging, which drives it toward 0 while acceptance stays low: with a wrong-signed gradient it reached $2 \times 10^{-7}$ in 11 iterations, about 5 million leapfrog steps each, and `tune` hung (code review of M6). With the cap, a step size below $10^{-3}$ gives trajectories shorter than 1, and HMC stays valid | raise when dual averaging shrinks the step size by some factor, which a transient of rejections can also do; no cap | adopted |
| 18 | 2026-10-23 | HMC's meeting step | The random-walk meeting step rejects a proposal with a non-finite gradient, as the trajectories do (spec §5.5) | as a gradient-free kernel whose states carry gradients it raised (spec §4.1), so a run failed by its seed when a meeting step proposed into a region that the trajectories reject (code review of M6). HMC as a whole now never moves to such a point | raise, as other gradient-free kernels whose states carry gradients do | adopted |
| 19 | 2026-10-23 | Kernel contract | `Kernel.check_target(target)` raises `ValueError` when the target lacks the log-density or the gradient that the kernel needs; `init_state` and the sampler's constructor call it (spec §5.1) | the sampler repeated `init_state`'s check and its message (code review of M6); one method keeps the rule in one place, and a custom kernel can extend it | a private helper; the check in `init_state` alone, which the sampler reaches only after tuning | adopted |
| 20 | 2026-10-23 | Interrupted results | Results record the indices they were asked for (`Replicates.requested`). `Result.interrupted` is true while a requested replicate has not run; `merge` joins the requests; a sampler's next run starts with the missing indices (spec §6.5, §7.1) | a run set the flag and `merge` kept it if either part had it, so a run that completed an interrupted one stayed flagged. With the requests recorded, the flag says exactly whether a set of indices fixed in advance ran, the condition for exact unbiasedness (D-22), and completion works on executors that start tasks in any order (altitude review of M4 and M5, deferred to M7) | a flag set by the run; a count of requested replicates, which assumes a prefix | adopted |
| 21 | 2026-10-23 | Pools | Pools that telescopic starts use the spawn method. A run is cut into chunks of consecutive indices, four per worker, all submitted at once; after the first Ctrl-C the chunks not started are cancelled, except the first. Tasks in the parent process, on thread pools, leave BLAS settings alone (spec §6.4, §6.5) | spawn behaves alike on Linux, macOS and Windows, while forking a process whose BLAS runs threads can deadlock (Python 3.12 warns); submitting every chunk lets a cluster executor use all its workers; BLAS limits belong to the process, and concurrent threads would restore each other's | fork on Linux; a bounded number of chunks in flight, which can leave a cluster's workers idle; a chunk size from a timing of the pilot | changed by #23 |
| 22 | 2026-10-23 | Budget mode | Deferred beyond v0.1 (spec §3.7, §12) | the per-lane estimator weights each replicate by its lane's count, which every estimate, interval and diagnostic of `Result` would have to carry, for a feature the specification marks should; an interrupted run and the run that completes it already give a stopping point that restores exact unbiasedness | implement it in-process in M7, as the implementation plan said | adopted; QUESTIONS asks Josh |
| 23 | 2026-10-23 | Pools, revised | The workers of an integer pool live for one call of `run` or `meeting_times`: they start before tuning and serve the pilot and the run, and the job travels with each task. Chunks number one per worker at least, more where a chunk would exceed 64 lanes, and four per worker at most. The first Ctrl-C reaches telescopic's workers through an event: they start no new replicate, and a later chunk runs nothing, except the first. A task in the main thread of another process pins BLAS and ignores Ctrl-C. The engine's monitor is the one channel for progress, slow pairs and the stop (spec §6.3, §6.4, §6.5) | measured on the M7 review's setups: `sample` of 200 replicates in 10 dimensions on 4 workers took 4.1 s and now 1.8 to 2.0 s; after a first Ctrl-C on 2 workers (4,096 replicates requested) the result came 13.6 to 15.6 s later, with 1,536 replicates, and now 0.85 to 1.15 s later, with 320 to 384. Chunks of 7 replicates cost 17.4 µs per replicate-iteration against 4.5 at 64 lanes; a user's process pool gave no result after one Ctrl-C; slow pilot pairs on a pool never warned (`notes/reviews/m7.md`) | a pool per run (#21); a pool kept for the sampler's life, which leaves idle processes and needs a close; four chunks per worker whatever the lanes | adopted |
| 24 | 2026-10-23 | Example targets | `examples.gaussian(d=10, rho=0.9)` ($\Sigma_{ij} = \rho^{|i-j|}$, from $\mathcal N(0, 4I)$), `banana()` (from $U[-5, 5]^2$), `mixture()` (JOA20's, from $\mathcal N(10, 10^2)$), `funnel(d=10)` (from $\mathcal N(0, I)$) and `pumps()` (from $U(0.001, 2)^{11}$, one Gibbs block per coordinate, exact moments by quadrature over $\beta$). An `Example` also carries `conditional` and `blocks` for Gibbs; the pump conditionals are a small gamma class rather than frozen SciPy ones (spec §9) | the initial distributions cover each target's mass; the published banana and mixture starts are kept; frozen SciPy distributions cost 400 µs to build, and a Gibbs step builds one per row and block (M7 review), so a tutorial-sized pump run would take a minute instead of a second | SciPy conditionals; one block for the ten rates, whose `logpdf` would not be a scalar | changed by #27 |
| 25 | 2026-10-23 | `check_coupling` | From `n` copies of `(x, y)`: Fisher's exact test of staying put, two-sample KS tests on 5 random projections and on the distance moved, Bonferroni-corrected, for each chain against a step alone; 100 pairs of equal states over 100 coupled steps; near misses below $10^{-9}$ of the positions' size. `passed(alpha=1e-3)` (spec §9) | an exact test needs no large-sample condition and handles chains that never stay; projections and the distance catch errors in direction and in scale; a recomputed meeting leaves positions apart by rounding only, which no distributional test sees | a chi-squared test of staying; all `n` pairs for faithfulness, which took 17 s for the 11-block Gibbs sampler | changed by #28 |
| 26 | 2026-10-23 | Saved results | One `.npz` per result: the replicates' arrays, the tuning arrays and the pilot as arrays, and the settings, names, seed, counts and the descriptions (`repr`) of the kernel and the initial distribution as one JSON string; schema 1; the engine's paths are not saved (spec §7.1) | `np.load(allow_pickle=False)` reads every field; JSON keeps a seed's entropy of any size exactly; a `Result` holds no paths | HDF5 (a dependency, and on the roadmap for checkpoints); pickling the kernel, which the spec excludes | changed by #29 |
| 27 | 2026-10-23 | Example targets, revised | The funnel starts from $\mathcal N(0, \Sigma)$, its exact covariance, and the pump model from $U(0.001, 5)^{11}$, so that every default initial distribution has at least the target's variance in each coordinate. `ndim` is a property, from `mean`. | 28 | 2026-10-23 | `check_coupling`, revised | From `n` copies of `(x, y)`: Fisher's exact test of staying put and KS tests on 5 projections and on the distance moved, as in #25; the fraction of pairs that met; the chains whose returned log-density or gradient differs from a fresh evaluation by more than $10^{-9}$ of its size; the pairs within 64 units in the last place in every coordinate. In one dimension a single projection is tested, since every projection gives the same test (code review) (spec §9) | the engine moves a pair that met with `step` alone, so faithfulness never reaches a result, and the suite tests it for the built-in kernels; a coupling that never meets gives no estimate; the engine reuses the cached log-density and gradient, so a stale one changes results while the positions' laws are right; a recomputed proposal differs from the original by rounding, a few units in the last place, while $10^{-9}$ of the size also counts pairs that a contracting coupling brings close from close starts | #25's 100 steps of 100 equal pairs; a relative near-miss threshold | adopted |
