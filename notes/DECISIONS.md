# Decisions

Decisions taken with Josh, or after the specification was written. Design decisions
taken while writing the specification are in its decision table
(`docs/design/spec.md`). Outcomes: **adopted**, **changed** (cite the commit),
**deferred**, **revisit**.

| # | Date | Topic | Decision | Reason | Alternatives | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-23 | Scope of v0.1 | Published coupling constructions only. New ones (e.g. a coupled ensemble sampler, coupled NUTS) are prototyped and validated in `notes/experiments/` and promoted only after Josh has seen the evidence (Josh) | keeps v0.1 on proven ground and potential papers under Josh's control | new methods as first-class v0.1 features; no new methods at all | adopted |
| 2 | 2026-10-23 | Process | One working branch (`claude/coupled-mcmc-package-btpupz`). Each milestone is a reviewed unit: tests, `/simplify`, `/code-review` at an effort scaled to its size, fixes, a log entry, then commit and push. No GitHub issues or pull requests (Josh) | less overhead for an autonomous first draft; the same review gates as a pull request | GSPICE-style tracking issues and a draft pull request per milestone | adopted |
| 3 | 2026-10-23 | Review budget | Consecutive milestones may form one review unit: one `/simplify` round and one `/code-review` on their combined diff, sized by the table in `CLAUDE.md`. The reuse and simplification angles may share one reviewer. M0 to M3 get one `/code-review` pass | about 14M tokens must cover nine more milestones; a reviewer's cost is mostly fixed (reading the spec, `CLAUDE.md` and the context), so fewer, larger units buy the same scrutiny for less | one unit per milestone with four reviewers each (DECISIONS #2 as first applied) | adopted |
| 4 | 2026-10-23 | Sampler settings | `max_iter` is fixed by a sampler's first `run`, like `k`, `m`, `lag` and `thin` (spec §8.1) | a `Result` has one cap, which its censoring and its warning name; replicates run under different caps would mix two censoring rules | record the cap per replicate | adopted |
| 5 | 2026-10-23 | Tuning interface | `Kernel.tune(target, initial, rng=None, *, pilot=None)`: `pilot` takes adaptive Metropolis chains already run, so a `Mixture` tunes its components on one pilot (spec §5.1) | a pilot costs 4 max(2000, 200d) evaluations, and every component would learn the same mean and covariance from it | `**options` as the spec first wrote; one pilot per component | adopted |
| 6 | 2026-10-23 | Multimodality warning | The tuning chains disagree when $\hat R > \sqrt 2$ in some coordinate (spec §5.3) | the spec's test, chain means spreading more widely than the chains, is between-chain variance above within-chain variance, which is $\hat R^2 > 2 - 1/n$; a convergence threshold such as 1.1 judges how well the pilot mixed, which this warning is not about | $\hat R > 1.1$ or $1.01$, the convergence rules for MCMC output | adopted; M9 measures its false-alarm rate |
| 7 | 2026-10-23 | Tuning pilot | The 4 adaptive Metropolis chains share one running mean and covariance (step $1/(t+1)$), started from the variances of 100 draws from $\pi_0$; each chain keeps its own global scale (step $(t+1)^{-0.6}$). A tuned random walk uses $2.38/\sqrt d$ (spec §5.3) | chains adapting alone at rate $t^{-0.6}$ did not converge for $d \ge 10$ ($\hat R$ 2 to 3 on $\mathcal N(0, I_{10})$), so $k$ came out 5 to 50 times too large, and at $d = 20$ the pilot pairs never met. Measured $k$ (100 pairs, 3 seeds, `notes/reviews/m4-m5.md`): median at most 1.3 times the exact covariance's for $d \le 20$, including variances from $10^{-4}$ to $10^2$, and 4.4 times at $d = 50$ | Stan's windowed re-estimation (never met on the badly scaled target at $d = 20$); an identity start (2 to 20 times the exact $k$ there, 15 to 33 times at $d = 50$); the adapted scale (median $k$ 1.1 to 1.2 times larger at $d = 20$) | adopted; M9 measures larger $d$ |
| 8 | 2026-10-23 | Sampler runs | `run(thin=None, max_iter=None)`: on the first run `None` means 1 and no cap; on later runs `None`, like `"auto"`, keeps the first run's value, and any other change raises. Settings are validated before the memory estimate and kept only once the first run succeeds. Each run ends by emitting the warnings of spec §7.4 for the cumulative result, except slow pairs and disagreeing tuning chains, emitted as they happen (spec §8.1) | with defaults of 1 and `None`, an explicit `thin=1` after `thin=2` was taken for "unchanged"; spec §6.3 requires a warning for censored pairs, and implementation plan §7 wants warnings by default | the settings in the constructor and `run(n)` alone (altitude review); warnings only from `check()` | adopted |
| 9 | 2026-10-23 | Merging results | `Result.merge` refuses results with different seeds | a replicate index identifies a replicate only under one root seed, and the merged result records one seed | allow it and record no seed | adopted |
| 10 | 2026-10-23 | Kernel contract | `step` returns each chain's acceptance probability instead of an accepted flag (spec §5.1) | dual averaging adapts to the mean acceptance probability (Hoffman & Gelman 2014), and four chains' flags would make it noisier; a flag is visible from the state anyway | keep flags and adapt on them; a separate method for tuning | adopted |
| 11 | 2026-10-23 | Gradient kernels outside the support | MALA and HMC refuse initial states with $\log\pi = -\infty$ (spec §4.2) | the proposal starts from the gradient there, which user code often makes NaN; a refusal names the point and the fix, where a rejection would leave the chain stuck | propose without drift from such states; reject and warn | adopted |
| 12 | 2026-10-23 | MALA's coupling | MALA has no `coupling` parameter: the reflection-maximal coupling is its only one (spec §5.4) | the specification offered no other value, and a parameter with one value is a no-op | a rejection maximal option for comparison, as `RandomWalk` has | adopted |
| 13 | 2026-10-23 | Dual averaging | Hoffman & Gelman's constants ($\gamma = 0.05$, $t_0 = 10$, $\kappa = 0.75$, $\mu = \log 10\epsilon_0$), 1000 iterations on 4 chains started from the RWMH pilot's last states, on their mean acceptance probability; $\epsilon_0 = 1.65\,d^{-1/6}$ for MALA and $d^{-1/4}$ for HMC; HMC adapts its trajectories without the meeting step (spec §5.4, §5.5) | published defaults; the pilot's last states are near stationarity, so the adaptation is not spent on a burn-in; over 5 seeds on a 5-dimensional Gaussian with condition number 100, the stationary acceptance was 0.572 to 0.608 for MALA (target 0.574) and 0.802 to 0.811 for HMC (target 0.8) | Stan's search for $\epsilon_0$; adaptation from draws of $\pi_0$ | adopted |
