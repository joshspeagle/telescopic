# M6 review: MALA, HMC, Gibbs, dual averaging

**Diff:** 07f0b80..b55f93a in `src/` and `tests/`, about 1,200 lines touching numerics
and the public API: three `/simplify` reviewers (DECISIONS #3), on altitude, efficiency,
and reuse with simplification. The `/code-review` follows these fixes.

## Outcome

- **Starts outside the support** (altitude). Gradient kernels refused initial points with
  log π = −∞ (DECISIONS #11). The refusal fired inside the engine at the first replicate
  that drew such a point, so a run failed or not by its seed and R, and lost every
  finished replicate. States there now carry a gradient of 0: MALA and HMC propose from
  them without drift and accept any proposal of finite density, as RWMH does
  (DECISIONS #14, with the reviewer's measurements). HMC's acceptance ratio moved inside
  its `errstate` block, where −∞ − (−∞) no longer warns.
- **`Kernel.needs_log_prob`** (altitude). The sampler allowed `log_prob=None` only for an
  instance of `Gibbs`, so it refused a mixture of Gibbs kernels, and nothing below the
  sampler checked: a random walk on `Target(None)` ran on log π ≡ 0. The kernel now says
  whether it needs a log-density, and the sampler and `init_state` check it against
  `Target.has_log_prob` (DECISIONS #15). The gradient check uses `Target.has_grad`.
- **Tuning** (altitude, reuse). `MALA._tuned` and `_Leapfrog._tuned` were one recipe
  with two constants, and dual averaging rebuilt the kernel at each of its 1,001 steps,
  validating and factoring Σ each time. A `GradientKernel` base now holds the step size,
  the covariance and the recipe; `with_step_size` copies a kernel without validating Σ
  again, and MALA keeps εC and C⁻¹/ε, computed once per step size. `dual_averaging` takes
  the pilot and starts from its last states; its 1,000 steps are a constant.
  `Mixture._tuned` returns a copy of itself, so `HMC` needs no override and keeps its
  class, and `HMC` reads `step_size`, `n_steps` and `cov` from its leapfrog component
  instead of holding them twice. `HMC(meeting_scale=-1)` names `meeting_scale`.
- **Gibbs** (altitude, reuse). `"crn"` is `"two-scale"` at threshold 0 and `"maximal"`
  is `"two-scale"` at threshold ∞, so one rule chooses the coupling. Conditionals are
  built only for the rows that use them; the `q` built for identical rows was never read.
  `"crn"` refuses an `eta` other than 1, as the other couplings refuse `threshold`
  (DECISIONS #16). The repr names `eta` and `threshold`.
- **Hot paths** (efficiency). HMC gathered and scattered rows at every leapfrog step even
  when every trajectory still moved; `evaluate_proposal` now takes the live rows and
  calls the target on the whole batch when all are usable, copying its arrays only when
  a gradient is bad, and the coupled halves are views. `acceptance()` replaced
  `nan_to_num` (9 µs a call) by `fmax`. A mixture whose uniforms choose one component
  for every row, always so at one lane, calls it directly. A Gibbs step builds its
  generators and a read-only view once per sweep. MALA's `np.where` on the ratio went:
  NaN and −∞ both reject.
- **Tests.** One light `Normal` in `tests/references.py` serves the coupling and kernel
  tests. New: the multivariate-normal CRN branch (shifting it by 0.5 passed the whole
  suite), chains entering the support from outside (both MALA and HMC), Gibbs kernels and
  their mixtures without a log-density, a mixture with a random walk refused, kernel
  names mapped to their classes, and `eta` refused with `"crn"`. The exact one-step law
  runs Gibbs at one gap: in one dimension the conditional ignores the state, so the
  other two gaps ran the same path. `Tagged.step` in the engine tests returns
  probabilities, the contract of DECISIONS #10. Each new test fails on the bug it
  targets (four checked by mutants).

Measured on the final code, b55f93a against it. Engine cost per pair-step on
N(0, I₁₀) from N(3, I), k = L = 141, m = 1410 (the reviewer's setting), best of 5
interleaved runs; tuning time of the step size alone, on a given pilot:

| Kernel | 1 lane | 256 lanes | `tune` (d = 10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| RWMH | 104.0 → 94.3 µs | 1.02 → 0.97 µs | |
| MALA (ε 1.164) | 134.8 → 114.4 µs | 2.00 → 1.81 µs | 173.9 → 82.6 ms |
| HMC (ε 0.826, 2 steps) | 222.9 → 152.8 µs | 3.25 → 2.26 µs | 195.3 → 118.4 ms |

Meeting times are identical in every configuration. Tuned HMC step sizes are bitwise
equal at d = 3, 10 and 30; MALA's differ by about 1e-15 relative at d = 10 and 30,
because C⁻¹/ε replaces the inverse of εC. Without the cached factors, MALA's step cost
9% more at d = 50 and 33% more at d = 200 on one lane, the case of a run's slow tail.
Setup: Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz, 4 cores (KVM), `threadpool_limits(1)`, Python 3.13.16,
NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1.

## Findings not adopted, or deferred

- Efficiency: MALA in whitened coordinates. With w = Cᵀ∇log π, the proposal
  x + εC(εw/2 + z) has forward residual z and needs no inverse: 3 matrix-vector products
  per step instead of 7. In the reviewer's prototype at d = 50 and 256 lanes, a step
  took 1,010 µs instead of 1,774, and a coupled step 2,209 instead of 3,710, the latter
  only with `reflection_maximal` returning its normals, a change to a coupling
  primitive. The arithmetic changes beyond the bit. M9's benchmarks will weigh it.
- Efficiency: storing Cᵀ contiguously saves 5 to 11% of a product at d = 10 to 50 and
  holds another d × d array; the results differ at 1e-15. Deferred to M9 with the item
  above, which removes most of these products.
- Efficiency: HMC validates and stores Σ twice, in its leapfrog and its meeting step:
  8 MB and 25 ms at d = 1,000, once per kernel. Sharing it needs a constructor path into
  `RandomWalk` that skips validation; not worth it at that cost.
- Reuse: resolving HMC's `n_steps` once in the constructor. The default ⌈1/ε⌉ must
  follow the step size through `with_step_size`, so a property derives it.
- Reuse: `ndim` and the `<dxd>` repr repeated in `RandomWalk`, `MALA` and `HMC`, and the
  acceptance formula repeated in `tuning.py` (an import cycle blocks the reuse). The base
  class now holds the gradient kernels' shared parts; the rest is two lines each.
- Reuse: sampler tests that `assert` a run's replicate count are smoke tests. The kernel
  name test now also checks the class.

## `/code-review` of M6

**Scope:** 07f0b80..4931ecb in `src/` and `tests/` (M6 and the fixes above), at high
effort. Ten findings: eight fixed, one answered with a stronger test, one fixed in its
documentation. Each source fix has a test that fails without it (eight checked by
mutants).

1. With `coupling="crn"`, a conditional with `ppf` for a block of several coordinates,
   such as `scipy.stats.norm` with a vector of means, got one uniform for all of them:
   perfectly dependent coordinates and a wrong law, without an error. The `ppf` branch
   now serves blocks of one coordinate; larger blocks need a multivariate normal, or
   raise `TypeError`.
2. HMC's default `n_steps` = ⌈1/ε⌉ grew without bound as dual averaging shrank ε:
   with a wrong-signed gradient, `tune` hung. The default is now at most 1,000
   (DECISIONS #17). A wrong gradient then yields a small step size rather than an
   error; a check of the gradient against finite differences is a candidate for M8's
   `check_coupling`.
3. HMC's meeting step, a random walk whose states carry gradients, raised on a
   non-finite gradient where the trajectories reject, so runs failed by their seed. It
   now rejects (DECISIONS #18); `RandomWalk._tuned` keeps the subclass, so the tuned HMC
   rejects too.
4. `step_size=inf` passed validation; HMC then took 0 leapfrog steps and raised
   `UnboundLocalError`. Step sizes and scales must now be finite.
5. `Kernel.tune` documented `rng` as the pilot's seed only, while dual averaging also
   draws from it. The docstring now says so. A mixture's gradient components share
   dual-averaging streams: tuning randomness never enters an estimate, so this stays.
6. Common random numbers pushed a uniform of exactly 0 (probability 2^-53) through
   `ppf`, giving −∞. Uniforms are now at least the smallest normal float.
7. `with_step_size` did not validate its argument and lacked a NumPy docstring.
8. The generic suite ran Gibbs only with state-independent conditionals and the maximal
   coupling, so the common-random-number coupling had no marginal, faithfulness or
   batch test. It now runs `"maximal"` and `"crn"` with a conditional that depends on
   the other coordinates, including the 3-dimensional comparison with single steps.
   `"two-scale"` joins these two couplings by a switch, which its own test covers.
9. The dual-averaging test's bound of 0.05 was not derived from a Monte Carlo error. It
   checks the outcome, the stationary acceptance at the tuned step size, which varies
   with the seed beyond the error of its 4,000-draw mean (0.0055): up to 0.034 above
   the goal over 5 seeds (DECISIONS #13). The comment now derives the bound from both.
   A new test compares `dual_averaging` on a kernel with deterministic acceptance
   exp(−ε) with an independent implementation of Hoffman & Gelman's algorithm, to
   1e-12; a change of κ from 0.75 to 0.7 fails it.
10. The sampler repeated `init_state`'s log-density check and message.
    `Kernel.check_target` now holds both checks, called by both (DECISIONS #19).
