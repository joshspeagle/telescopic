# M8 review: saving, plotting, examples, `check_coupling`

**Diff:** 0d70b85..bfaf26c in `src/` and `tests/`, over 500 lines touching the public API:
three `/simplify` reviewers (DECISIONS #3), on altitude, efficiency, and reuse with
simplification. The `/code-review` follows these fixes.

## Outcome

- **`check_coupling` checks what the engine uses** (altitude). It tested faithfulness,
  which no result depends on: the engine moves a pair that met with `step` alone
  (`engine.py:335`). It missed two things that results depend on. The reviewer
  confirmed both with runs:
  - a coupling whose Y carries X's log-density passed, and its runs were biased (z from
    -3.9 to -2.8 on all 8 estimates of E[x²]);
  - a coupling that never meets passed, and censored all 50 pairs of a run.

  A correct Gibbs kernel with `eta=0.8` failed. The report now counts the pairs that met,
  the chains whose cached log-density or gradient differs from a fresh evaluation, and
  near misses within 64 units in the last place. The old relative threshold of 10⁻⁹
  failed a correct random walk on a target of scale 10⁻¹⁰. The target is evaluated once
  at x and y rather than at n copies of each (efficiency). The suite's 3-D marginal test
  of every built-in coupling now calls `check_coupling`, and the duplicate in
  `test_testing.py` went (reuse). New tests make the stay test fail and make
  `passed()` fail on a near miss alone (DECISIONS #28).
- **Joint histograms** (altitude, reuse). The corner plot binned pairs with code of its
  own (`_cells`) and showed no errors. `Result.histogram` now takes one coordinate or
  several, with standard errors, and the corner plot draws from it. The `Result.plot_*`
  methods take the axes to draw on, as the plotting functions do (DECISIONS #30).
- **Saved results** (altitude, reuse). The module is private (`telescopic._io`), so that
  `from telescopic import io` no longer shadows the standard library. A result without
  a kernel no longer loads the string `"None"`. The seed is saved as
  `SeedSequence.state`, which also keeps `n_children_spawned`. `Result.load` is a class
  method. The round-trip test compares every attribute of the result, so a new one that
  saving drops fails it (DECISIONS #29).
- **Examples** (altitude, reuse, efficiency).
  - The funnel starts from N(0, Σ) and the pump model from U(0.001, 5)¹¹. The old starts
    were narrower than the targets: the funnel's v had variance 1 against 9, and
    U(0.001, 2) left out 73% of the posterior of β and 32% of that of λ₁₀ (400,000
    draws).
  - `ndim` is a property, from `mean`.
  - The pump conditionals compute in Python numbers. `fsum` and `lgamma` change last
    bits, so seeded pump runs change draw by draw, not in law.
  - The Gaussian's log-density and gradient use two-operand `einsum`s. The gradient
    was `x @ P`, whose bits changed with the batch in 510 of 512 rows, against spec
    D-20; the bit test below now catches that (DECISIONS #27).
- **Example tests** (reuse). The Gaussian's draws came from its own covariance, so its
  moment tests could not fail. They now come from the AR(1) recursion. Each
  log-density is compared with SciPy's density of the generative description, up to a
  constant. The banana's covariance is checked by quadrature, and the funnel's against
  the lognormal mean. The moment tests follow the test plan's escalation rule, through
  a helper in `tests/references.py`. Batch and point evaluations must agree bit for bit.
- **Plots** (efficiency, reuse).
  - The survival curve is drawn at 0 and its jumps only, and reads the stopping times
    from the result.
  - The TV bound is drawn at no more than 4,000 times, with its legend at the upper
    right instead of the costly "best".
  - Plot tests now compare the drawn values with the result: bar heights, error boxes,
    the corner mesh's sums against the marginals, the survival curve with censored
    pairs, the TV bound, and the histogram's lag shift.
- **Gibbs docstring** (altitude). It states the cost of frozen SciPy conditionals, one
  built per row and block, and points to the small class of `telescopic.examples`.

Measured on Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz, 4 cores, Python 3.13.16, NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1,
matplotlib 3.11.2, against bfaf26c. Each figure is the best of 2 to 5 repeats; the plots
ran interleaved with bfaf26c's plotting code in one process:

| | bfaf26c | now |
|---|---|---|
| Gibbs coupled step, pump model, 64 rows (two runs) | 10.2 and 10.9 ms | 6.8 and 6.9 ms |
| Gibbs step, pump model, 64 rows (two runs) | 2.6 and 2.7 ms | 1.7 and 1.7 ms |
| Gaussian log-density, d = 10, 512 rows | 87.3 µs | 38.3 µs |
| Gaussian gradient, d = 10, 512 rows | 5.6 µs (510 rows change bits with the batch) | 30.8 µs (none) |
| TV bound plot, max τ − L = 10⁶ | 1.16 s, 98 MB | 0.09 s, 32 MB |
| TV bound plot, max τ − L = 10⁷ | 11.4 s, 962 MB | 0.71 s, 320 MB |
| meeting-time plot, max τ − L = 10⁷ | 9.5 s, 1,763 MB | 0.13 s, 5 MB |
| corner plot, d = 10, R = 1,000, 1.0 × 10⁶ atoms | 2.3 to 2.4 s, 160 MB | 4.4 to 5.4 s, 96 MB |

The corner plot is slower because each pair now bins both coordinates and computes
standard errors (82 ms per pair). Its densities are bitwise those of bfaf26c. A frozen
SciPy gamma takes 248 µs to build, and a normal 294 µs (best of 5 × 2,000).

## Findings not adopted, or deferred

- Altitude: the Stein identity E[∇log π(x) xᵀ] = −I was proposed as the link between
  each log-density and its draws. A consistent 10 → 12 typo in the banana survived it
  (z ≈ 2.4 at 20,000 draws). The exact comparison with SciPy's densities caught all
  five density mutants, so it replaces the Stein test.
- Altitude, deferred to before M10:
  - `plotting.paths(replicates, lag)` needs a lag that nothing checks, and the engine's
    private namespace constant.
  - `Replicates` should record k, m, the lag, thin, `max_iter` and the root seed. Then
    `Result(replicates)`, `paths(replicates)` and `merge` need no settings.
  - `engine.run` should take `rng`, as the spec's §8.2 does.
- Altitude: `check_coupling` inspects one pair of starting points with identical rows.
  It sees neither errors confined to pairs unlike (x, y) nor a row's dependence on the
  batch (R-KER-6). The caller chooses the points, and the suite tests batch invariance
  for the built-in kernels.
- Efficiency: the corner plot's pair masses by one `bincount`, without per-replicate
  masses. Superseded: the pairs come from `Result.histogram`, whose errors need them.
- Efficiency: `ks_2samp`'s asymptotic method would save about 90 ms per check, but
  moves the p-values in the third decimal. It would be a decision without a measured
  need.
- Efficiency: the pump reference draws need 10 sweeps of burn-in, not 50; the tests
  use 20, for a margin.
- Reuse: mutants that remove the Bonferroni factor, the guard for fewer than two moves,
  or the seed's `pool_size` survive. Low value: none changes a correct kernel's
  verdict, and no test uses another pool size.
- Reuse: the 1-D branch of `plotting.paths` runs in no test. It is a teaching plot.
