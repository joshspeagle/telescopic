# M9 review: the slow statistical suite, the benchmarks and the warnings

**Diff:** c69b90a..3293f6a in `src/`, `tests/` and `benchmarks/`, about 550 lines: three
`/simplify` reviewers (DECISIONS #3), on altitude, efficiency, and reuse with
simplification. The `/code-review` follows these fixes.

## Outcome

- **The benchmark script could never finish** (efficiency, altitude). Its last case,
  `sample(kernel="hmc")` on the banana, raises when more than 1% of the pilot pairs are
  unmet at the cap (QUESTIONS, HMC's automatic step), and the JSON was written only at the
  end. The script now records the error, writes after each section and creates
  `benchmarks/results/`, which git does not keep while empty.
- **The benchmarks measure spec section 10** (altitude, reuse). They used k = 10, m = 100,
  per kernel application, with BLAS free. They now run the M3 review's setup (coupled
  random walk on N(0, I₁₀) from N(3, I), k = L = 141, m = 1410), per pair-step, with BLAS
  on one thread, and add the point-by-point overhead beyond the log-density at one lane
  and the per-replicate random streams. The pipeline overhead is measured at d = 10, 50
  and 100 from `Result.wall_time` instead of a second run of the engine. The setup records
  `threadpool_info()` and `git describe --dirty`. Small lane counts run 16 replicates
  instead of 1,024.
- **The tuning pilot beyond 20 dimensions** (efficiency, altitude). The 10d² steps of
  DECISIONS #34 stored 160d³ bytes, 4.3 GB at d = 300, and factorised the covariance at
  every step, d⁵ in all. The chains now refactor it and keep their states every ⌈d/20⌉
  steps: at d = 100, 18 s and 32 MB instead of 25 s and 160 MB, with k 1.42 and 1.57
  times the exact covariance's (`m9_pilot_length_3.out`). Below 21 dimensions nothing
  changes. The altitude reviewer's alternative, 4⌈d/20⌉ chains of 200d steps, was measured
  (2.5 to 3 times faster for a vectorised target, similar conditioning) and is recorded in
  #34 as an option: it gains nothing for a log-density evaluated point by point and
  changes the report's shape.
- **Evidence for #34** (altitude). The d = 100 run that motivated it had not finished, and
  the condition numbers cited by #34 and the d = 50 tuning test were in no committed
  output. `m9_pilot_length_2.out` now has both, at d = 15, 50 and 100.
- **The slow suite** (reuse, simplification, efficiency).
  - It uses `references.gaussian_log_prob` for the standard normal, a `pairs` helper
    with a lag for the TV test and the four reproductions, and `stats.sem`.
  - Arguments equal to their defaults are gone from the unbiasedness cases. The
    literature reproductions keep the papers' settings, and say so.
  - The exact TV curve is computed once, up to the first time below 0.01, which is exact
    because a chain's distance to stationarity never increases: 47 quadratures instead
    of 200.
  - The two conjugate models share one `Conjugate` with the prior drawn by its own
    `rvs`, an observation function, a log-likelihood and a threshold.
  - The coverage test runs batches of 4,000 replicates instead of 20,000, a fifth of the
    memory, and cites the streaming mode of spec §6.6. `AR1` builds its factors once.
  - The HMC start is a constant whose comment cites the HMC question.
  - 18 slow tests pass in 142 s (150 s before).
- **Tuning tests** (reuse). The whitened condition number is one helper on
  `scipy.linalg.eigh(cov, pilot.cov)`.

## Answered, not changed

- **Timed runs store their atoms** (efficiency). Spec §10's lockstep target is for the
  main-run settings, which store; the pilot case runs without storing, as pilots do.
- **An exact-moments helper** (reuse). The suite computes them once; the experiment scripts
  are records of past runs and keep their own lines.
- **`sample()` cannot set the lanes** (altitude). At the default R = 100 its 64 lanes leave
  36 replicates for a second wave. Outside this diff; left for the performance work of M11.

## Found while running the studies

- **The README example** (DECISIONS #35). From the spec's N(0, 10I), 70 posterior standard
  deviations away, one replicate held more than half the variance in 11 of 20 runs, and
  some runs estimated a negative variance. The README now starts from a Gaussian ten times
  wider than an optimiser's fit: no warning in 20 runs, 14 times faster. A question asks
  Josh about remedies that are new constructions.
- **The pump model** needs k near 29,000 with the automatic random walk, 3 minutes per run,
  so the warning study leaves it out.
- **The variance warning** (DECISIONS #36). At a quarter it fired on 87 of 200 clean banana
  runs at R = 100, whose intervals covered as often as the others. It now needs more than
  half: 19 of 200, where the intervals of E[x₀²] covered at 0.84 against 0.96. It still
  fires on every run of JOA20's bimodal failure whose replicates found the other mode, and
  on 11 of 20 runs of the README's line fit from N(0, 10I), the same runs as before. A
  test pins the threshold from both sides. The spec records the measured rates of every
  warning (§7.4).

## Code review (high)

One reviewer in one context, without the separate verification pass. No correctness bug;
six findings, all fixed in acb12f9:

- **The pilot's thinning had no test of its contract.** Three mutants passed every test: an
  acceptance rate over the kept states only (0.50 instead of 0.25 at d = 21), a stride
  that left one state per chain in a short pilot (R-hat NaN), and kept states not ending
  with the chain's last. Two tests at d = 21 now catch the first two; the third would need
  a spy on the chains, and would only move the start of dual averaging by a few steps.
- **The docstrings** said that states are kept every ⌈d/20⌉ steps, but a short pilot keeps
  them closer. They now say so, and one variable serves the refactoring and the kept
  states.
- **The far starts** of the slow suite are centred three standard deviations away; the
  comment said every coordinate starts at least that far.
- **The benchmark** recorded the default BLAS threads rather than the one its cases ran
  with, and not the CPUs this process may use. It records both.
- **`exact_tv`** had one caller and is inlined.

## Benchmarks

`benchmarks/results/acb12f9.json`, on an idle 4-CPU Xeon at 2.1 GHz with BLAS on one
thread. Against spec §10:

- lockstep main run at 256 lanes: 1.27 µs per pair-step, target ≤ 2 µs, met; 1.09 µs at
  1,024 lanes and 87.9 µs at one; the pilot 3.81 µs at 256 lanes;
- one lane with a log-density evaluated point by point: 90.7 µs per pair-step beyond the
  log-density, target ≤ 40 µs, missed as at M3 (93 µs);
- per-replicate random streams: 0.28 µs per lane-step and 41 µs per replicate's set-up,
  targets 0.5 and 50 µs, met;
- a 1 ms log-density on 4 workers: 1.98 times faster, target 3.5, missed; the 6.3 s job
  includes starting the workers, so a longer job or a started executor would measure the
  throughput instead, which the performance work of M11 should do;
- the pipeline's pilots: 31%, 37% and 39% of the main run at d = 10, 50 and 100,
  target 15%, missed;
- automatic kernels: on the 10-dimensional Gaussian, inefficiency 1.18 (random walk, k =
  202), 1.14 (MALA, k = 18) and 1.15 (HMC, k = 92); on the banana, the random walk gives
  1.12 (k = 1,417), and MALA and HMC cannot choose k, their pilot pairs unmet at 10^5
  (QUESTIONS).
